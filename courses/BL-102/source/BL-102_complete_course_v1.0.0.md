## 4. Full lessons

### BL-102-L01 — Set Clear Expectations
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Objective:** Explain what successful work looks like.

**Why this matters**
Employees cannot reliably meet expectations they have not heard or understood.

**Lesson content**

**Make work observable**
Replace “keep the store nice” with specific tasks such as checking shelf labels, removing empty cartons, and clearing aisle hazards.

**Define the standard**
Explain what needs doing, when it is due, and how to know it is complete. Ask employees to repeat the plan in their own words when helpful.

**Keep standards consistent**
Use the same core expectations for similar roles. Adjust tasks for training, ability, accommodation, and authorized responsibilities as appropriate.

**Hardware-store example**
Before opening, a supervisor asks an associate to check the fastener aisle, remove empty packaging, and report missing shelf labels by 9:30.

**What to say or do**
Say: “Please check the fastener aisle before 9:30 and tell me which labels need replacing.”

**Common mistakes**
Giving vague instructions; assuming understanding; changing priorities without telling the team.

**When to ask for help**
Ask the store manager or HR contact about accommodations, policy interpretation, or unclear job responsibilities.

### BL-102-L02 — Coach in the Moment
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Objective:** Use specific, respectful feedback to improve performance.

**Why this matters**
Small corrections made early are easier to act on than vague criticism later.

**Lesson content**

**Describe what happened**
Focus on observable behavior rather than labels about a person. “The customer waited while you searched” is more useful than “You are careless.”

**Explain the effect**
Connect the behavior to safety, customer experience, or team workflow. Invite the employee to describe what happened.

**Agree on a next step**
Demonstrate the expected action, invite practice, and follow up. Recognize improvement when you see it.

**Hardware-store example**
An employee leaves a customer without explaining they are checking inventory. A supervisor privately models a short update and practices it with the employee.

**What to say or do**
Say: “When you need to check stock, tell the customer what you are doing and when you’ll return.”

**Common mistakes**
Correcting someone publicly when privacy is possible; making assumptions about intent; offering no next step.

**When to ask for help**
Use established HR procedures for repeated misconduct, discrimination concerns, or serious safety violations.

### BL-102-L03 — Delegate with Follow-Through
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Objective:** Assign work without losing accountability.

**Why this matters**
Delegation creates capacity only when employees have the information and authority to succeed.

**Lesson content**

**Match task and readiness**
Consider training, workload, and safety authorization. Do not delegate regulated or hazardous work to an unqualified employee.

**Clarify the handoff**
State the outcome, deadline, resources, decision limits, and check-in time. Give employees room to complete the task within those boundaries.

**Close the loop**
Review completion, remove obstacles, and recognize progress. Avoid repeatedly taking the task back unless safety or urgency requires it.

**Hardware-store example**
A manager delegates an endcap refresh to a trained associate, shares the approved planogram, sets a noon check-in, and identifies who can approve substitutions.

**What to say or do**
Say: “Use this approved layout. Let me know by noon if an item is unavailable.”

**Common mistakes**
Delegating without a deadline; assigning work without authority; micromanaging every step.

**When to ask for help**
Escalate staffing constraints, restricted equipment use, and decisions outside the employee’s role.

### BL-102-L04 — Handle Concerns and Conflict Respectfully
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Objective:** Respond fairly to team disagreements and concerns.

**Why this matters**
Unresolved conflict can affect service, safety, and trust.

**Lesson content**

**Listen separately when needed**
Hear each person’s account without assuming who is right. Ask about specific actions and dates, not rumors.

**Use the right channel**
Handle routine task disagreements with coaching. Follow HR and reporting policies for harassment, discrimination, threats, retaliation, or other serious concerns.

**Agree on practical next steps**
Clarify expectations, document as required by policy, and check whether the problem is resolved. Protect confidentiality to the extent appropriate.

**Hardware-store example**
Two associates disagree over who should restock a display. The supervisor reviews assignments and workload, listens to both, and clarifies the next shift’s responsibilities.

**What to say or do**
Say: “I want to understand what happened and clarify the work so this does not repeat.”

**Common mistakes**
Taking sides before hearing facts; discussing private concerns with unrelated coworkers; ignoring serious reports.

**When to ask for help**
Immediately involve the designated manager or HR contact for threats, harassment, discrimination, or safety risks.

**Answer:** B. Fact-finding and clear expectations support fair resolution.

### BL-102-L05 — Lead a Consistent Daily Huddle
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Objective:** Use short team check-ins to align priorities.

**Why this matters**
A few minutes of clear communication can prevent repeated questions and missed tasks.

**Lesson content**

**Start with priorities**
Share the day’s key customer-service goals, deliveries, promotions, and safety reminders. Keep the message short.

**Invite useful questions**
Ask what could block progress and who needs support. Confirm which employee owns each priority.

**Review and adjust**
At the end of the shift, note what was completed and what needs handoff. Do not share confidential employee or customer information in a group huddle.

**Hardware-store example**
A morning huddle covers an incoming truck, a weekend paint promotion, and a blocked receiving aisle. The team assigns owners and a check-in time.

**What to say or do**
Say: “Our priorities are a clear receiving aisle, accurate sale signs, and helping customers quickly. What might get in the way?”

**Common mistakes**
Listing too many priorities; using huddles to embarrass staff; skipping follow-up.

**When to ask for help**
Escalate unsafe conditions, staffing shortages, or unresolved customer issues to the responsible leader.
