## 4. Individual lessons

### RF-103-L01: Spot Hazards Before They Cause Problems
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes  
**Learning objective:** Identify common store hazards and take a safe first step.

#### Why this matters
A small problem can become a serious injury. A loose box, blocked aisle, or wet floor can affect customers and coworkers.

#### Lesson content

**Look at the path**
Watch for spills, loose products, cords, and clutter. Keep walkways and exits clear.

**Recognize changing conditions**
A busy delivery or crowded display can create a new hazard. Check your area as work changes.

**Protect the area**
If a hazard is safe to address within your training, follow store procedures. If not, keep people away and alert the right person.

**Do not improvise**
Do not handle unknown chemicals, damaged electrical items, or unstable loads without proper training and instructions.

#### Hardware-store example
You notice a carton in a main aisle. A customer is walking toward it. You warn the customer, protect the area, and move the carton only if it is safe and allowed by store procedures.

#### What to say or do
- Stay alert while walking through the store.
- Warn others about an immediate hazard.
- Follow store procedures to remove or isolate the hazard.

#### Common mistakes
- Walking past a hazard without reporting it.
- Trying to clean an unknown spill without knowing the substance.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a supervisor about hazards you cannot safely fix. Get help for unknown spills, damaged wiring, and unstable merchandise.

### RF-103-L02: Lift, Move, and Store Merchandise Safely
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes  
**Learning objective:** Use safe judgment with loads and storage.

#### Why this matters
Hardware stores carry heavy, sharp, and awkward products. A rushed move can hurt someone or damage merchandise.

#### Lesson content

**Plan before lifting**
Check the item's size, shape, and path. Decide whether you need help or approved equipment.

**Use a stable movement**
Keep a secure grip and avoid sudden twisting. Do not carry a load that blocks your view.

**Ask for help**
Use team lifting or approved material-handling equipment when a load is too heavy or awkward for you.

**Store with care**
Keep heavy merchandise secure. Do not create unstable stacks or block aisles, exits, or emergency equipment.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer needs a large bag of concrete. The bag is awkward for you to move alone. You arrange help or use approved equipment instead of forcing the lift.

#### What to say or do
- Check the load and the route.
- Ask for help before lifting an awkward item.
- Keep storage areas orderly and stable.

#### Common mistakes
- Lifting beyond your ability to save time.
- Stacking products where they could fall.

#### When to ask for help
Ask for help with heavy, sharp, oversized, or unstable items. Follow store rules for carts, ladders, and lifting devices.

### RF-103-L03: Know Your Limits with Tools and Equipment
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes  
**Learning objective:** Recognize tasks that need training or authorization.

#### Why this matters
Stores may use ladders, cutters, powered equipment, and service tools. Familiar equipment can still cause injuries.

#### Lesson content

**Use only authorized equipment**
Complete required training before using powered equipment or specialized store tools.

**Check before use**
Follow the store's inspection steps and the manufacturer's instructions. Report damaged tools or missing guards.

**Respect restricted areas**
Stay outside marked work zones. Do not distract someone who is operating equipment.

**Stop when unsure**
Do not guess about equipment operation or attempt repairs beyond your training.

#### Hardware-store example
A coworker asks you to use a powered pallet jack. You have not been trained or authorized. You decline and contact a qualified employee.

#### What to say or do
- Check whether you are authorized.
- Follow the equipment instructions and store procedures.
- Report damage and stop unsafe work.

#### Common mistakes
- Using a machine because you have watched someone else use it.
- Removing a guard to finish a job faster.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a supervisor before using unfamiliar equipment. Only trained and authorized people should perform restricted tasks.

### RF-103-L04: Respond to Spills, Injuries, and Emergencies
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes  
**Learning objective:** Take appropriate first actions and follow the store plan.

#### Why this matters
Fast, calm action can protect people. Every store needs clear procedures for incidents and emergencies.

#### Lesson content

**For immediate danger**
Move away from danger and alert others. Contact emergency services when needed, using local procedures.

**For an injury**
Notify the designated person. Get emergency help for serious injuries. Do not provide care beyond your training.

**For a spill**
Keep others away. Identify the substance only through safe, approved methods. Follow the store's spill procedure and safety information.

**For an alarm or evacuation**
Follow the store's emergency plan. Use designated exits and assembly points. Do not block emergency responders.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer falls near a display and says they cannot stand. You alert the designated employee and request emergency help as appropriate. You do not move the customer unless immediate danger makes it necessary.

#### What to say or do
- Know how to alert a manager and emergency responders.
- Keep others away from danger.
- Follow the store emergency plan.

#### Common mistakes
- Assuming someone else called for help.
- Moving an injured person without a safety reason or proper training.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a manager for the store's emergency contacts, evacuation plan, and incident process. Emergency services should handle urgent medical or fire emergencies.

### RF-103-L05: Speak Up and Report Safety Concerns
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes  
**Learning objective:** Describe hazards and near misses clearly.

#### Why this matters
Reporting gives the team a chance to fix a problem before someone gets hurt. A near miss is an event that could have caused harm but did not.

#### Lesson content

**Report promptly**
Tell the appropriate person about hazards, injuries, damaged equipment, and near misses.

**Be specific**
Describe what happened, where it happened, and what immediate action you took.

**Share facts**
Do not guess about causes or blame people. Use the store's reporting process.

**Follow through**
Confirm that the concern reached the right person. Do not assume a report means the area is now safe.

#### Hardware-store example
A stack of buckets shifts and nearly falls. No one is hurt. You keep the area clear and report the location, what moved, and what you observed.

#### What to say or do
- Report near misses as well as injuries.
- Give clear facts and a precise location.
- Keep the area protected until the hazard is addressed.

#### Common mistakes
- Ignoring a near miss because nobody was hurt.
- Guessing about the cause instead of sharing observations.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a manager which reporting form or channel your store uses. Escalate an unresolved immediate hazard right away.
