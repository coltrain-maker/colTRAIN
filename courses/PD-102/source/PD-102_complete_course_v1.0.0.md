## 4. Full lessons

### PD-102-L01: Discover the Project Before the Product
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Objective:** Lead a useful paint conversation with project questions.

**Why this matters**
The customer may ask for a specific product before describing the actual job.

**Begin with the surface**
Ask what is being painted, whether it is new or previously coated, and whether it is indoors or outdoors.

**Understand the result**
Ask what matters most: appearance, cleaning, exposure, timing, or budget. Do not assume the most expensive option is best.

**Confirm constraints**
Find out whether the customer has tools, how soon the area must be used, and whether there are damaged or damp surfaces.

**Hardware-store example**
A customer requests “bathroom paint.” You learn the issue is recurring moisture and peeling. You avoid promising paint alone will fix the cause.

**What to say or do**
Ask: “What is happening on the wall now, and has the moisture problem been addressed?”

**Common mistakes**
Starting with price; recommending a coating before asking about surface condition.

**When to ask for help**
Refer unresolved moisture, suspected mold, or unknown previous coatings to appropriate help.

### PD-102-L02: Estimate Paint Quantities Responsibly
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Objective:** Explain how to estimate coverage and why actual use varies.

**Why this matters**
Too little paint delays a project. Too much may create unnecessary expense.

**Measure the area**
For a rectangular wall, multiply width by height to estimate square footage. Add wall areas for the full project. Openings can be accounted for when practical.

**Check product coverage**
Use the coverage range printed on the chosen product or its official calculator. Surface texture, porosity, application method, and number of coats affect the result.

**Explain the estimate**
A coverage figure is an estimate, not a promise. Round purchases based on available container sizes and the customer’s project needs.

**Hardware-store example**
A customer has two walls measuring 10 feet wide by 8 feet high. You calculate 160 square feet before considering doors, windows, and coats. You then check the selected product’s coverage.

**What to say or do**
Say: “This is the wall area. Let’s check this paint’s stated coverage and the number of coats your project needs.”

**Common mistakes**
Using one universal coverage rate; forgetting multiple coats; guaranteeing the estimate.

**When to ask for help**
Ask a specialist for unusual textures, spray application, or large commercial estimates.

### PD-102-L03: Compare Paint Options Without Pressure
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Objective:** Use customer priorities and verified claims to compare choices.

**Why this matters**
Customers benefit from a clear explanation of value, not a sales pitch.

**Compare relevant features**
Review intended use, sheen options, preparation requirements, coverage, and manufacturer-stated performance.

**Explain price in context**
A lower shelf price may not reflect the full project cost if preparation or extra coats differ. Do not promise fewer coats unless the specific product supports that claim.

**Offer a choice**
Present two appropriate options and explain a meaningful difference. Let the customer decide.

**Hardware-store example**
A customer is repainting a high-traffic hallway. You compare two suitable wall paints using their published cleaning guidance and prices.

**What to say or do**
Say: “Both are labeled for interior walls. This one has a different cleaning claim. Would you like to compare the label details?”

**Common mistakes**
Calling one product “best” without context; inventing durability claims.

**When to ask for help**
Escalate warranty, special-use, and performance claims you cannot verify.

**Optional unscored practice**

### PD-102-L04: Build the Right Supply List
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Objective:** Identify needed preparation, application, and cleanup items.

**Why this matters**
A good sale helps the customer complete the project without avoidable return trips.

**Think through the steps**
Consider cleaning, repairs, masking, protecting nearby surfaces, priming if needed, applying, and cleaning up.

**Match tools to the task**
Brushes, rollers, trays, extension poles, drop cloths, tape, and abrasives vary by job. Use the coating label for compatible applicators.

**Recommend, do not bundle blindly**
Explain why each item may be useful. Check what the customer already owns.

**Hardware-store example**
A customer buys paint for a small bedroom and already owns a roller frame. You ask whether they have the right cover, a tray, a brush for edges, and surface protection.

**What to say or do**
Say: “What tools do you already have? We can fill in only what is missing.”

**Common mistakes**
Adding unrelated items; forgetting cleanup or surface protection.

**When to ask for help**
Ask for help with specialty coatings, spray equipment, or respiratory protection requirements.

**Optional unscored practice**
PD-102-L04-P01. What is the best way to recommend add-on supplies?

**Answer:** B. Relevant supplies support project success; unnecessary items do not.

### PD-102-L05: Close with a Project Recap
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Objective:** Confirm the plan and help the customer leave prepared.

**Why this matters**
A clear final recap reduces confusion after the customer gets home.

**Repeat the plan**
Summarize the surface, selected product, estimated quantity, key preparation, and application guidance.

**Point to trusted instructions**
Show the label or official technical guidance for timing, ventilation, and cleanup. Do not replace those instructions with guesses.

**Invite follow-up**
Tell the customer how to get more help if an unexpected condition appears before application.

**Hardware-store example**
A customer chooses paint for a previously painted door. You recap cleaning, checking for damage, label-based prep, and appropriate application tools.

**What to say or do**
Say: “Before you go, here is what we selected and what the label says to do first.”

**Common mistakes**
Ending immediately after payment; promising a specific finish result.

**When to ask for help**
Escalate if the customer describes bubbling, widespread peeling, or unexplained dampness.
