## Employee-facing content

### 1. Today’s skill

Distinguish the basic uses of a drill/driver and an impact driver.

### 2. Why it matters

Both tools can drive screws, so customers may assume they do the same job. Explaining their basic differences helps customers understand why a combination kit includes both.

### 3. Learn it

**A drill/driver is a general-purpose drilling and screwdriving tool. An impact driver is primarily a fastening tool.**

| Tool | Basic use | Common identifying feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Drill/driver** | Drilling holes and driving screws | An adjustable chuck with jaws that tighten around a compatible bit |
| **Impact driver** | Driving fasteners, including demanding screwdriving tasks | A quick-change holder commonly accepting compatible ¼-inch hex-shank accessories |

A drill/driver turns the bit continuously. Many models have an **adjustable clutch** for screwdriving. The clutch limits the turning force delivered at the selected setting, helping control how deeply a screw is driven. It does not guarantee that a screw cannot be overdriven. Follow the model’s instructions for selecting drilling or screwdriving settings. [Source 1]

An impact driver adds **repeated twisting blows** when its impact mechanism operates. These rotational impacts help with demanding fastening work. That makes it useful for tasks such as driving longer screws, subject to the tool’s and fastener manufacturer’s instructions. [Source 2]

**Recommended action:** Start by asking, “Are you mainly making holes, driving fasteners, or doing both?” Use the answer to explain the tools before comparing individual models.

**Common mistake:** Saying a drill cannot drive screws or an impact driver cannot drill holes. Their capabilities overlap. Some impact drivers can drill with suitable accessories, but accessory fit, ratings, and permitted applications must be checked. [Source 3]

**When to verify or seek help:** Check the exact tool manual and accessory instructions before recommending a bit or application. Confirm that accessories are suitable for impact use when applicable. If compatibility is unclear, ask a knowledgeable coworker or manufacturer representative.

### 4. Customer scenario

A customer is looking at a kit containing a drill/driver and an impact driver. They plan to make holes in wood and install screws.

They ask, “Why would I want two tools that look so similar?”

### 5. Quick check

**Question ID: CD-0006-Q01**

Which explanation best describes their basic roles?

A. The drill/driver handles drilling and screwdriving; the impact driver primarily drives fasteners using rotational impacts.
B. The drill/driver only makes holes; the impact driver is required for every screw.
C. The impact driver is primarily for drilling; the drill/driver is primarily for fastening.
D. Both tools work the same way; their main difference is the bit-holder shape.

### 6. Take it to the floor

Ask **“holes, fasteners, or both?”** before explaining which tool fits the customer’s work.
