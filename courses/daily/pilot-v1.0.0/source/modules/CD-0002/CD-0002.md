## Employee-facing content

### 1. Today’s skill

Distinguish four common plumbing connection types using their joining features, without assuming that similar-looking parts are compatible.

### 2. Why it matters

A customer may call every connection that turns a “screw-on fitting.” Recognizing how the connection actually joins helps you ask better questions before selecting a replacement.

### 3. Learn it

Start with **how the parts join**, rather than the fitting’s color or overall shape.

| Connection type | Recognition clue |
|---|---|
| **Threaded pipe connection** | External threads on one part screw into matching internal threads on another. |
| **Slip joint** | Common on tubular sink drains: a nut tightens around a tube, with a washer providing the seal. |
| **Solvent weld** | A plastic pipe fits into a socket designed for joining with the appropriate solvent cement. |
| **Push-to-connect** | A compatible pipe pushes into a fitting with an internal gripping and sealing mechanism. |

Threaded pipe connections and solvent-welded connections use different joining methods. Solvent welding chemically joins compatible plastic surfaces; it is not simply a friction fit. [Source 1]

**Recommended action:** Identify the connection at **each end**. One adapter can have a slip-joint connection at one end and a solvent-weld socket at the other. Naming one end does not describe the entire fitting. [Source 2]

**Common mistake:** Seeing threads on a slip-joint nut and treating it as a threaded pipe connection. Look at the whole assembly: the tube, nut, and sealing washer.

Push-to-connect fittings may hide their working parts. For example, SharkBite describes an internal grab ring that holds the pipe and an O-ring that seals it. Do not assume every push fitting accepts the same pipe materials. [Source 3]

**When to verify or seek help:** Before recommending a replacement, confirm pipe material, size, connection type, and intended use against the specific product’s instructions. A smooth socket alone does not prove that a fitting is intended for solvent welding. If identification remains uncertain, ask a knowledgeable coworker or manufacturer representative.

### 4. Customer scenario

A customer brings in a loose nut and washer from beneath a bathroom sink. Their photo shows a smooth drain tube passing through the nut into another fitting.

They ask, “What kind of connection should I look for?”

### 5. Quick check

**Question ID: CD-0002-Q01**

Which connection type best matches the assembly shown and described?

A. Slip joint
B. Threaded pipe connection
C. Solvent weld
D. Push-to-connect

### 6. Take it to the floor

Name the connection at each end before matching the replacement. Connection type is one part of the match—not proof of compatibility.

## Reviewer and manager materials

### Answer key — separate from employee-facing content

- **Question ID:** CD-0002-Q01
- **Correct answer:** A. Slip joint
- **Explanation:** A smooth tubular sink drain passing through a nut with a sealing washer identifies a slip-joint assembly. The nut has threads, but the tube itself is not screwed into matching pipe threads. The described assembly does not use a solvent-weld socket or an internal push-to-connect gripping mechanism.
- **Teaching reference:** Section 3, “Learn it,” connection table and “Common mistake.”
