## Employee-facing content

### 1. Today’s skill

Identify the information a coworker needs to continue an unfinished customer task.

### 2. Why it matters

A customer’s request may outlast your shift. A clear handoff helps the next employee continue the work without making the customer start over or guessing what was promised.

### 3. Learn it

**A useful handoff explains where the task stands and what needs to happen next.**

Use these five prompts:

| Prompt | Information to include |
|---|---|
| **What is the request?** | The customer’s goal and relevant product or order reference |
| **What is already done?** | Completed checks, actions, and confirmed findings |
| **What is still open?** | Missing information or an unresolved decision |
| **What happens next?** | The specific next action and any actual commitment already made |
| **Who is taking over?** | The person accepting responsibility, or the fact that acceptance is still needed |

**Recommended action:** Communicate the handoff in a way the receiving coworker can access and understand. Options include a direct conversation, a written note, a shared shift log, an internal message, or a note in an existing customer or order record. Follow store procedures where they exist.

For a verbal handoff, ask the receiving coworker to confirm the next action. If you leave a note or send a message, confirm they received it and accepted responsibility rather than assuming the task has transferred.

Keep the handoff short and factual. Separate confirmed information from anything that still needs checking. For example, “Supplier availability not yet confirmed” is clearer than “Should be here soon.”

Include a follow-up time only when one was actually agreed upon. Share customer details only with coworkers who need them, using a method appropriate for that information.

**Common mistake:** Writing “Please handle this” without identifying the next action or confirming who will take it. A person’s name in a note does not establish that they have accepted the task.

When possible, confirm that the receiving coworker understands and accepts the handoff. If no one has accepted it, use the store’s usual method, or ask a supervisor if no process is established, instead of treating the task as transferred.

**When to verify or seek help:** Check the order record or original message when a price, product detail, or customer commitment is uncertain. Ask a supervisor when responsibility remains unclear. Reference existing customer records where available, using the store’s usual method, or ask a supervisor if no process is established. Avoid copying unnecessary personal details into a general shift note.

### 4. Workplace scenario

Your shift is ending. A customer has asked whether a replacement mower belt can be ordered.

You recorded the mower model and requested a supplier availability check. No supplier response has arrived, no price or arrival date has been promised, and Jamie has agreed to continue the task.

### 5. Quick check

**Question ID: CD-0010-Q01**

Which note gives Jamie the most useful handoff?

A. “Mower belt customer needs help. Details should be somewhere in the system.”

B. “Jamie—replacement belt will probably arrive soon. Please call the customer.”

C. “Belt request recorded under the customer’s service inquiry, including mower model. Supplier availability check requested; reply pending. Price and arrival date unconfirmed; neither promised. Jamie accepted follow-up: review the supplier response and update the customer through the inquiry record.”

D. “Supplier contacted about a mower belt. Task complete.”

### 6. Take it to the floor

Leave the next person with **the current status, the next action, and clear ownership**.

## Reviewer and manager materials

### Answer key — separate from employee-facing content

- **Question ID:** CD-0010-Q01
- **Correct answer:** C.
- **Explanation:** This note identifies the request record, completed action, unresolved information, limits of customer commitments, next action, and accepted ownership. The other notes omit essential information, introduce an unsupported expectation, or incorrectly mark unfinished work complete.
- **Teaching reference:** Section 3, “Learn it,” five-prompt table, “Recommended action,” and “Common mistake.”

### 7. Supporting materials and sources

**Actual supplied media assets:** None.

**Suggested optional material:** An independently created handoff card with five fields: request, completed work, open items, next action and commitments, and receiving person.

**Accessibility description for proposed media:** “A handoff card contains five clearly labeled text fields covering the request, completed work, unresolved information, next action and existing commitments, and the person accepting responsibility.” Provide the card as readable text with a clear reading order.

**Sources and authorship:** This is an independently authored retail communication framework proposed for colTRAIN. No external framework, technical standard, manufacturer requirement, or proprietary curriculum is claimed.

**Verification needs:** Participating stores should identify practical handoff methods, customer-information practices, and a person to ask when responsibility is unclear; no established handoff location or process is assumed. The service inquiry record and Jamie’s acceptance are fictional scenario details, not assertions about a store’s systems or policies.
