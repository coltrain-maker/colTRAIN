## Employee-facing content

### 1. Today’s skill

Identify which label markings describe voltage, electric current, and electrical power.

### 2. Why it matters

Electrical labels can place several numbers close together. Knowing what each unit means helps you explain the label without confusing a product’s voltage with its current or power.

### 3. Learn it

**Read the unit beside the number.** The number alone does not tell you what is being measured.

| Label marking | What it measures | Plain-language meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **V — volts** | Voltage | The electrical potential difference, sometimes described as electrical “push.” |
| **A — amperes, or amps** | Electric current | The rate at which electric charge flows. |
| **W — watts** | Power | The rate at which energy is transferred or used. |

These units describe different quantities. They are related, but their numbers are not interchangeable. [Sources 1 and 2]

**Recommended action:** Read both the number and its unit aloud: “This marking says eight amps.” Then check the surrounding label text to understand what that rating describes.

For a device that uses an adapter, pay attention to **input** and **output**. Input describes what the device or adapter receives; output describes what it supplies. Do not combine numbers from different parts of a label.

**Common mistake:** Calling every electrical number “wattage.” A number followed by **A** is an amperage marking, even when a wattage marking appears nearby.

Watts also differ from **kilowatt-hours (kWh)**. Watts describe power; kilowatt-hours describe energy used over time. A wattage number alone does not tell you the total energy a customer will use. [Source 3]

**When to verify or seek help:** If a marking is missing, unclear, or unfamiliar, check the exact model’s manufacturer instructions. Identifying these units does not establish that a cord, adapter, outlet, or circuit is suitable. Refer compatibility questions to the product documentation and unresolved wiring or circuit questions to a qualified electrician.

### 4. Customer scenario

A customer shows you a product label with several electrical markings. One line reads **“Rated current: 8 A.”**

The customer asks, “What does that line tell me?”

### 5. Quick check

**Question ID: CD-0003-Q01**

What does the **8 A** marking describe?

A. The product’s rated voltage
B. The product’s rated power
C. The product’s energy use over eight hours
D. The product’s rated electric current

### 6. Take it to the floor

Read the number, unit, and surrounding label text together before explaining an electrical rating.

## Reviewer and manager materials

### Answer key — separate from employee-facing content

- **Question ID:** CD-0003-Q01
- **Correct answer:** D. The product’s rated electric current
- **Explanation:** A stands for amperes, commonly called amps, which measure electric current. Voltage is measured in volts, and power is measured in watts. The marking does not describe energy use over a period of time.
- **Teaching reference:** Section 3, “Learn it,” unit comparison table and “Common mistake.”
