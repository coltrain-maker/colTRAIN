## Employee-facing content

### 1. Today’s skill

Distinguish common paint finishes by their relative shine and identify when to check the product’s sheen guide.

### 2. Why it matters

Customers may choose a paint color without realizing they also need to choose a finish. Understanding sheen helps you explain how shiny the dried paint will look.

### 3. Learn it

**Sheen describes the shine of dried paint.** Color and sheen are separate choices: naming a color does not identify its finish.

Use this general progression to explain common finish names:

| Finish | General appearance |
|---|---|
| Flat or matte | Little to very little visible shine |
| Eggshell | A soft, low shine |
| Satin | More noticeable shine than eggshell |
| Semi-gloss | A distinctly shiny finish |
| Gloss or high-gloss | The shiniest end of the range |

This is a guide to relative appearance, not a universal measurement chart. Flat and matte can be separate finishes. Manufacturers may also use names such as pearl, low lustre, or soft gloss. Check the selected product’s guide before treating those names as equivalent. [Source 1]

**Recommended action:** Ask, “How much shine would you like?” Then use the manufacturer’s finish guide or an available physical sheen sample to clarify the choices.

**Common mistake:** Promising cleaning performance from the sheen name alone. The paint formulation also matters; some low-sheen products are designed for washability. Confirm the specific product’s claims before making that promise. [Source 2]

**When to verify or seek help:** Check the product label, manufacturer’s guide, or technical data sheet when comparing unfamiliar finish names or answering performance questions. If the information does not resolve the question, ask a knowledgeable coworker or manufacturer representative.

### 4. Customer scenario

A customer has selected a wall color. Within the same paint line, the store offers flat, eggshell, satin, and semi-gloss.

The customer says, “I want a little shine—more than flat, but less than satin.”

### 5. Quick check

**Question ID: CD-0001-Q01**

Which finish best matches the customer’s description?

A. Semi-gloss
B. Flat
C. Eggshell
D. Satin

### 6. Take it to the floor

Use finish names to explain relative shine. Use the specific product’s information to confirm performance.

## Reviewer and manager materials

### Answer key — keep separate from employee-facing content

- **Question ID:** CD-0001-Q01
- **Correct answer:** C. Eggshell
- **Explanation:** Eggshell generally has a soft shine above flat and below satin. Flat does not provide the requested increase in shine; satin is the finish the customer wants less shine than; semi-gloss is farther toward the shiny end.
- **Teaching reference:** Section 3, “Learn it,” finish comparison table.
