## Employee-facing content

### 1. Today’s skill

Identify which light-bulb label measurement describes light output and which describes electrical power use.

### 2. Why it matters

Customers often ask for a bulb by its familiar wattage, such as “a 60-watt bulb.” With different lighting technologies, wattage alone does not reliably tell you how much light a bulb produces.

### 3. Learn it

**Use lumens to compare light output. Use actual watts to compare electrical power use.**

For example, an illustrative package might show **“Brightness: 800 lumens”** and **“Energy Used: 9 watts.”** These numbers describe different things.

| Label information | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Lumens** | How much visible light the bulb produces—the brightness measure used on packaging |
| **Actual watts** | The electrical power the bulb uses while operating |
| **“60-watt replacement” or similar wording** | A comparison with a traditional incandescent bulb, rather than the replacement bulb’s actual wattage |

The FTC explains that an LED can produce approximately the same light output as a traditional incandescent bulb while using fewer watts. Check the actual package rather than assuming every replacement has identical specifications. [Source 1]

**Recommended action:** When a customer asks for a brighter bulb, find the **brightness in lumens** on the package or Lighting Facts label. Compare that figure with the bulb they are replacing. More lumens means greater total light output; it does not guarantee identical lighting in every fixture. [Source 2]

**Common mistake:** Comparing an LED’s actual wattage with an incandescent replacement claim as though both numbers describe brightness. A package saying “60-watt replacement” may list a much lower actual wattage.

**When to verify or seek help:** Brightness is only one part of choosing a bulb. Before recommending a specific replacement, check its base, shape, voltage, and suitability for the fixture and controls against manufacturer information. If compatibility is unclear, ask a knowledgeable coworker or manufacturer representative.

Do not treat the replacement-equivalent number as the bulb’s actual electrical rating.

### 4. Customer scenario

A customer brings two bulb packages to the counter:

- **Bulb A:** 800 lumens; 9 watts.
- **Bulb B:** 1,100 lumens; 11 watts.

They ask, “Which one produces more light?”

### 5. Quick check

**Question ID: CD-0013-Q01**

Which answer uses the correct label information?

A. Bulb A, because its lower wattage means it produces more light.
B. They produce the same amount of light because both are LEDs.
C. Bulb B, because its higher wattage is the brightness measurement.
D. Bulb B, because 1,100 lumens is more light output than 800 lumens.

### 6. Take it to the floor

For **“How much light?”**, look at lumens. For **“How much electrical power?”**, look at actual watts.

## Reviewer and manager materials

### Answer key — separate from employee-facing content

- **Question ID:** CD-0013-Q01
- **Correct answer:** D. Bulb B, because 1,100 lumens is more light output than 800 lumens.
- **Explanation:** Lumens describe light output. Bulb B has the higher lumen rating, so it produces more total light. Watts describe electrical power use; selecting the correct bulb for the wrong reason does not demonstrate the skill.
