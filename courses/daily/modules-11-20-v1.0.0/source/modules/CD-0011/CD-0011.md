## Employee-facing content

### 1. Today’s skill

Explain what a paint coverage statement means and identify conditions that can change actual coverage.

### 2. Why it matters

A customer may read a coverage number as a promise that one container will finish the project. Understanding the statement helps you explain what the number includes before discussing how much paint to buy.

### 3. Learn it

**A coverage statement describes how much surface a stated quantity of paint can cover under specified conditions.** It may also be called the **spread rate**.

For example, a label might read **“Coverage: 300–400 square feet per gallon, per coat.”** This means one gallon is estimated to cover that much surface with one coat under the stated conditions. Look for the area, paint quantity, and any wording such as **“per coat.”**

*This is an illustrative example; check the actual product’s coverage statement.*

Read these details together:

| Detail | What to check |
|---|---|
| **Surface area** | Is coverage stated in square feet or square meters? |
| **Paint quantity** | Is the rate per gallon, per liter, or for the entire container? |
| **Application basis** | Does it specify one coat, a recommended film thickness, or other conditions? |

**One coat means one application over the surface.** A statement of coverage per coat does not mean that one coat will produce the finished result the customer needs. Manufacturer guidance distinguishes surface coverage from the additional paint that may be needed for more coats. [Source 1]

Actual coverage can change with:

- **Texture:** Rough or textured surfaces can require more paint.
- **Porosity:** Absorbent surfaces can take up more paint.
- **Application method:** Brushing, rolling, and spraying can produce different product requirements.

Manufacturer coverage guidance identifies surface conditions and application method as factors affecting paint quantity. [Sources 2 and 3]

**Recommended action:** Read the exact product’s label or technical data sheet. Explain the stated quantity, area, and application basis before treating the figure as a starting point for an estimate.

**Common mistake:** Confusing **coverage** with **hiding**. Coverage concerns the area painted at the specified application. Hiding concerns how well the dried paint conceals the surface or color underneath. A surface can be painted without the previous color being fully hidden. [Source 4]

**When to verify or seek help:** Check product instructions when coat requirements, surface preparation, or the coverage basis are unclear. Ask a knowledgeable coworker or manufacturer representative rather than promising the highest stated coverage.

### 4. Customer scenario

A customer’s project has 300 square feet of wall surface. Their selected paint’s label states **“Coverage: 300–400 square feet per gallon, per coat.”**

They ask, “Does that mean one gallon is guaranteed to finish my walls, even if they need two coats?”

### 5. Quick check

**Question ID: CD-0011-Q01**

Which response best explains the statement?

A. Yes—the coverage range includes every coat needed to finish that area.
B. No—the rate applies to each coat; additional coats require additional paint, and actual coverage depends on conditions.
C. Yes—300 square feet is the lower number, so surface conditions cannot affect the quantity.
D. No—the coverage statement measures how well the paint hides the old color, rather than the area it paints.

### 6. Take it to the floor

Read **area + paint quantity + application basis** before explaining a coverage number.
