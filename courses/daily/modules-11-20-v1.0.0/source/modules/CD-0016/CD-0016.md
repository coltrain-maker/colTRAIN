## Employee-facing content

### 1. Today’s skill

Distinguish common accessory shanks and recognize that fitting a tool’s holder does not establish suitability for use.

### 2. Why it matters

Two bits may have similar working ends but different ends that fit into the tool. Recognizing that connection helps you narrow the choices before checking whether the accessory suits the job.

### 3. Learn it

**The shank is the end of the bit held by the tool or accessory holder.** It is different from the working end that drills the hole or engages the screw.

For example, a Phillips driver bit can have a **¼-inch hex shank**. “Phillips” describes the screw-engaging tip; “¼-inch hex” describes the tool connection.

Start with these common shank types:

| Shank type | Recognition clue | Connection to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| **Round** | A cylindrical gripping section | A compatible adjustable-jaw drill chuck |
| **Hex** | Six flat sides | A matching hex holder or compatible drill chuck |
| **SDS-plus** | A specially grooved shank | An SDS-plus tool holder |

Bosch documents separate SDS-plus and three-jaw chucks for different accessory shanks. These are distinct connection systems, rather than interchangeable names for the same holder. [Source 1]

**Recommended action:** Ask which tool the customer has, including its model when available. Match the specified holder type and capacity to the accessory’s shank information.

Then check the accessory’s intended use. **Physical fit is only one check.** Confirm suitability for the tool’s operating action, the material, and any stated limits.

**Common mistake:** Assuming every hex-shank bit is suitable for an impact driver. A hex shape describes the connection; it does not, by itself, establish an impact rating. Look for the manufacturer’s stated compatibility. DEWALT’s guidance for drilling with an impact driver specifies impact-ready bits. [Source 2]

**When to verify or seek help:** Check the exact tool manual and accessory packaging when the holder, rating, or application is uncertain. Some short insert bits require a compatible holder; do not assume every bit attaches directly. Ask a knowledgeable coworker or manufacturer representative rather than recommending an unverified adapter or combination.

### 4. Customer scenario

A customer brings a driver bit with a six-sided shank and says, “It fits into my impact driver, so that means it’s made for impact use, right?”

The packaging is missing, and you have not identified the bit’s manufacturer or model.

### 5. Quick check

**Question ID: CD-0016-Q01**

What is the most accurate response?

A. Yes—every six-sided shank establishes an impact rating.
B. Yes—if it locks into the holder, no further compatibility checks are needed.
C. No—the shank’s fit does not establish an impact rating; identify the bit and verify its stated compatibility.
D. No—impact drivers cannot use hex-shank accessories.

### 6. Take it to the floor

**Match the connection, then verify the rating and application.**

## Reviewer and manager materials

### Answer key — separate from employee-facing content

- **Question ID:** CD-0016-Q01
- **Correct answer:** C. No—the shank’s fit does not establish an impact rating; identify the bit and verify its stated compatibility.
- **Explanation:** The shank establishes a connection type, not every aspect of suitability. Without identifying the accessory, the employee cannot confirm its impact compatibility. Physical fit alone is insufficient.
- **Teaching reference:** Section 3, “Learn it,” “Recommended action” and “Common mistake.”
