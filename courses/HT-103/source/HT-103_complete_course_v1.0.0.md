### HT-103-L01: Recognize Common Power Tools
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Match common power tool categories to jobs.

#### Why this matters
Customers may ask for a tool by its appearance instead of the task it performs.

#### Lesson content

**Drilling and driving**
Drills create holes with suitable bits. Drivers install or remove compatible fasteners. Impact drivers are a distinct category with different operating characteristics.

**Cutting and shaping**
Circular saws and jigsaws cut suitable materials with the correct blade. Sanders remove or smooth surface material. Grinders have different hazards and uses.

**Power sources**
Corded tools use mains electricity. Cordless tools use compatible battery systems. Do not assume batteries, chargers, or accessories are interchangeable.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer wants a tool to assemble furniture. You ask what fasteners are included and whether the instructions recommend a hand tool or powered driver.

#### What to say or do
- Ask what task the tool must perform.
- Identify the material.
- Check accessory and power requirements.
- Show the relevant tool category.

#### Common mistakes
- Calling every driver a drill.
- Assuming all batteries fit all tools.

#### When to ask for help
Refer tool-specific operation and hazardous applications to manufacturer instructions or a qualified specialist.

### HT-103-L02: Compare Corded and Cordless Options
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Explain practical differences without unsupported claims.

#### Why this matters
The best power source depends on the work area and the tool requirements.

#### Lesson content

**Corded tools**
Corded tools need an appropriate electrical supply and may suit work where a suitable outlet is available. Cord management is part of safe use.

**Cordless tools**
Cordless tools use a battery and charger approved for the tool. Runtime varies by task, battery, and tool. Do not promise a fixed runtime without verified data.

**Ask useful questions**
Where will the tool be used? Is power available? How often will it be used? Does the customer already own a compatible battery platform?

#### Hardware-store example
A customer needs a drill for projects around a large property. You ask whether outlets are nearby and whether they already own batteries in a particular tool system.

#### What to say or do
- Ask about access to power.
- Check existing battery systems.
- Compare manufacturer-listed specifications.
- Explain tradeoffs without making guarantees.

#### Common mistakes
- Assuming higher voltage always means the better tool.
- Mixing unapproved batteries and chargers.

#### When to ask for help
Ask for help with electrical supply, battery compatibility, or tool selection for hazardous environments.

### HT-103-L03: Understand Bits, Blades, and Accessories
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Recognize that accessories must match the tool and material.

#### Why this matters
An incorrect accessory can damage a project or create serious injury risk.

#### Lesson content

**Match the connection**
Drill bits, saw blades, and other accessories use specific shanks, mounts, or arbor sizes. Check the tool and accessory documentation.

**Match the material**
A blade or bit suitable for wood may not be suitable for masonry or metal. Verify intended materials and limitations on packaging.

**Check operating limits**
Accessories may have speed or other ratings. Never recommend an accessory that exceeds or conflicts with tool instructions. Guards and safety devices must remain in place.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer brings a photo of a circular saw and asks for a blade. You ask for the exact model and check the blade diameter, arbor, material, and tool instructions.

#### What to say or do
- Find the exact tool model.
- Check mounting dimensions.
- Verify intended material and operating limits.
- Escalate uncertain matches.

#### Common mistakes
- Choosing a blade by diameter alone.
- Suggesting removal of a guard to fit an accessory.

#### When to ask for help
Escalate all unclear accessory fit, speed ratings, and guard questions to manufacturer documentation or a qualified specialist.

### HT-103-L04: Teach Safety Before Features
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Explain basic power tool safety and limits of retail advice.

#### Why this matters
Power tools can cause serious injuries. Sales advice must not replace training or instructions.

#### Lesson content

**Read instructions first**
Manufacturer manuals explain guards, personal protective equipment, setup, and operation. Never tell customers to bypass safeguards.

**Prevent accidental starts**
Tools must be handled according to manufacturer guidance when changing accessories, transporting, or storing them. Unplugging or removing the battery may be required depending on the tool and task.

**Recognize high-risk jobs**
Electrical, structural, overhead, and unfamiliar cutting tasks may require qualified help. Retail associates should not provide unverified operating or installation instructions.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer asks whether they can remove a saw guard for a difficult cut. You explain that safety devices should not be bypassed and refer to the tool manual and a qualified professional.

#### What to say or do
- Point customers to the manual.
- Discuss required protective equipment as specified.
- Never suggest defeating guards.
- Stop and refer high-risk questions.

#### Common mistakes
- Demonstrating an unfamiliar tool without authorization.
- Treating safety labels as optional.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a supervisor before any in-store demonstration; refer complex or high-risk operation to trained professionals.

### HT-103-L05: Help Customers Choose and Maintain Tools
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Guide a responsible purchase and service handoff.

#### Why this matters
A complete recommendation includes the tool, compatible accessories, and realistic care needs.

#### Lesson content

**Match the project and frequency**
Ask what tasks the customer will do and how often. Compare manufacturer-listed capacity and included items.

**Check the box**
Some tools are sold without batteries or chargers. Verify package contents and compatible accessories before checkout.

**Support care and service**
Explain that cleaning, storage, inspection, and maintenance follow the owner’s manual. Do not invent warranty coverage or service promises.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer selects a cordless drill kit. You check whether the box includes a battery, charger, and bits. You explain where to find the owner’s manual and store service information if applicable.

#### What to say or do
- Ask about intended jobs.
- Verify box contents.
- Confirm compatible accessories.
- Refer maintenance and warranty questions to verified documentation.

#### Common mistakes
- Assuming a battery is included.
- Guaranteeing a warranty outcome.

#### When to ask for help
Refer uncertain repairs, service eligibility, and warranty coverage to the store manager or manufacturer.

#### Supporting materials
