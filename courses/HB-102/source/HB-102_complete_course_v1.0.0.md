### HB-102-L01: Recognize Common Plumbing Parts (5 minutes)

**Objective:** Identify common pipes, fittings, and valves

**Why this matters:** Correct product identification begins with understanding what each part does.

#### Know the basics
Pipes carry water or drain waste. Fittings join or change the direction of pipe runs. Valves control flow. Supply lines connect fixtures to water supplies.

#### Check before recommending
Common fitting shapes include elbows, tees, couplings, and adapters. An elbow changes direction. A tee creates a branch. A coupling joins similar ends. An adapter changes connection types.

#### Put it into practice
Names help locate a part, but do not prove that it fits. Size, material, pressure rating, connection type, and use must also match.

**Hardware-store example:** A customer holds a curved fitting and asks for another. Identify it as an elbow, then ask what material and connection style the existing system uses.

**What to say or do:** Say: “Is this for a water supply, drain, or another use? Do you have the old part or its markings?”

**Common mistakes:** Choosing a fitting by shape alone.

**When to ask for help:** Escalate unclear applications, unusual materials, or safety-critical systems to an experienced specialist.

**Key takeaways:**
- Pipes carry water or drain waste.
- Common fitting shapes include elbows, tees, couplings, and adapters.
- Names help locate a part, but do not prove that it fits.

### HB-102-L02: Understand Pipe Materials and Connections (5 minutes)

**Objective:** Distinguish materials and joining methods

**Why this matters:** A fitting must suit the pipe material and connection method.

#### Know the basics
Common plumbing materials include copper, PEX, PVC, and CPVC. Their permitted uses and joining methods differ. Do not assume all plastic pipes are interchangeable.

#### Check before recommending
Connections may be threaded, solvent-welded, crimped, clamped, compression, or push-to-connect. Each system has manufacturer instructions, compatible components, and limits.

#### Put it into practice
Identify the material and read the product labeling before making a recommendation. Local code requirements and installation conditions may also apply.

**Hardware-store example:** A customer asks for glue to connect two pieces of plastic pipe. Ask whether the pipe is PVC or CPVC and check the product instructions rather than choosing by bottle color.

**What to say or do:** Ask: “What material is printed on the pipe, and is this for supply water or drainage?”

**Common mistakes:** Assuming one cement or fitting works for every plastic pipe.

**When to ask for help:** Ask a qualified plumber or manufacturer support about unmarked pipe, transitions, or code-restricted uses.

**Supporting material:** Photo of labeled copper, PEX, PVC, and CPVC samples — **[Needed; not embedded]**. Placement: after lesson content. Alt text draft: Photo of labeled copper, PEX, PVC, and CPVC samples. Licensed image/technical verification required.

**Key takeaways:**
- Common plumbing materials include copper, PEX, PVC, and CPVC.
- Connections may be threaded, solvent-welded, crimped, clamped, compression, or push-to-connect.
- Identify the material and read the product labeling before making a recommendation.

### HB-102-L03: Measure Plumbing Parts Correctly (5 minutes)

**Objective:** Ask for the measurements that matter

**Why this matters:** Nominal pipe sizes and actual dimensions can differ.

#### Know the basics
Plumbing parts may use nominal sizes. The number on the label may not equal a ruler measurement across the outside of the pipe. Different systems use different sizing conventions.

#### Check before recommending
Check the printed size and material markings when possible. Ask for the old fitting or a clear photo showing markings. A measured outside diameter can help identify a system but should not be the only evidence.

#### Put it into practice
Connections also vary by male or female threads and thread style. A size match without the correct connection style can still fail.

**Hardware-store example:** A customer measures a pipe with a tape and requests a fitting. Ask for pipe markings and the connection type before choosing.

**What to say or do:** Say: “Pipe sizes can be named differently from their measured diameter. Let’s confirm the system.”

**Common mistakes:** Treating a ruler measurement as the product’s nominal size.

**When to ask for help:** Escalate uncertain thread types, mixed materials, and specialty connections.

**Key takeaways:**
- Plumbing parts may use nominal sizes.
- Check the printed size and material markings when possible.
- Connections also vary by male or female threads and thread style.

### HB-102-L04: Help With Common Fixture Questions (5 minutes)

**Objective:** Collect useful details before selecting a part

**Why this matters:** A leak symptom may have several possible causes.

#### Know the basics
Customers may ask about faucets, toilets, showerheads, drains, and supply connections. Ask which fixture is involved, where the issue appears, and whether the customer has the model number.

#### Check before recommending
A running toilet might involve more than one component. A dripping faucet may use a specific cartridge. Replacement parts can be model-specific.

#### Put it into practice
Do not promise a repair based on symptoms alone. Help identify a likely product category and verify the manufacturer’s compatibility guidance.

**Hardware-store example:** A customer needs a faucet cartridge. Ask for the faucet brand and model or a photo of the existing cartridge.

**What to say or do:** Say: “Many cartridges look alike. Let’s identify the fixture before selecting one.”

**Common mistakes:** Promising a universal cartridge will fit.

**When to ask for help:** Refer uncertain repairs, hidden leaks, or water damage to a qualified plumber.

**Key takeaways:**
- Customers may ask about faucets, toilets, showerheads, drains, and supply connections.
- A running toilet might involve more than one component.
- Do not promise a repair based on symptoms alone.

### HB-102-L05: Recognize Plumbing Safety and Escalation (5 minutes)

**Objective:** Identify when a question is beyond retail advice

**Why this matters:** Some plumbing problems risk flooding, contamination, or injury.

#### Know the basics
Active leaks, sewage backups, possible cross-connections, and water-heater issues may require prompt professional attention. Gas lines and gas-fired appliances are not general retail repair topics.

#### Check before recommending
Employees should not advise customers to defeat safety devices or use products outside their listed applications. Potable-water products need appropriate approval for drinking-water contact.

#### Put it into practice
Help customers find information and qualified support. For urgent flooding or immediate hazards, follow emergency and store procedures.

**Hardware-store example:** A customer reports water flowing from a ceiling near a light fixture. Recognize both water and electrical hazards; do not give do-it-yourself instructions.

**What to say or do:** Say: “That situation may be unsafe. Please seek qualified help promptly rather than trying a part at random.”

**Common mistakes:** Focusing on selling a fitting while ignoring a serious hazard.

**When to ask for help:** Escalate gas, sewage, water-heater safety, backflow, and combined electrical/water hazards.

**Key takeaways:**
- Active leaks, sewage backups, possible cross-connections, and water-heater issues may require prompt professional attention.
- Employees should not advise customers to defeat safety devices or use products outside their listed applications.
- Help customers find information and qualified support.
