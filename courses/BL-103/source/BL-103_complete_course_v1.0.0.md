## 4. Full lessons

### BL-103-L01 — Recognize Priorities During a Shift
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Objective:** Identify which tasks need attention first.

**Why this matters**
Hardware stores have customer questions, deliveries, and planned work happening at the same time.

**Lesson content**

**Safety comes first**
A spill, blocked emergency exit, or other immediate hazard needs prompt action under store procedure. Do not walk past a hazard to finish routine stocking.

**Consider urgency and importance**
An urgent issue needs attention soon. Important work supports service and operations even when no one is asking about it right now.

**Confirm the order**
When priorities compete, tell the manager what is happening and ask which task should come next.

**Hardware-store example**
You are restocking screws when you notice liquid on the floor. Secure the area and follow spill procedures before returning to the shelf.

**What to say or do**
Say: “I found a spill. I’m following the safety procedure, then I’ll return to stocking.”

**Common mistakes**
Treating the longest task as most important; ignoring hazards; silently dropping assigned work.

**When to ask for help**
Ask for help with hazardous spills, injuries, or competing demands you cannot resolve safely.

### BL-103-L02 — Plan the Next Useful Step
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Objective:** Turn a broad task into manageable actions.

**Why this matters**
A clear next step helps employees start and finish work.

**Lesson content**

**Define the outcome**
Instead of “fix receiving,” identify the specific result, such as sorting today’s delivery into labeled staging areas.

**Break work into steps**
Check instructions, gather supplies, complete one section, and verify the result. Use a short checklist when several details matter.

**Estimate realistically**
Ask about deadlines and available time. Do not promise a completion time without considering interruptions and resources.

**Hardware-store example**
An associate is told to prepare an endcap. They check the approved display plan, gather products and labels, build the display, and request a final check.

**What to say or do**
Say: “I’ll gather the products and labels first, then set the display and verify the prices.”

**Common mistakes**
Starting without instructions; doing easy tasks instead of required tasks; skipping verification.

**When to ask for help**
Ask a manager when the assignment, display rules, or available time is unclear.

### BL-103-L03 — Handle Interruptions Without Losing Your Place
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Objective:** Respond to customers while keeping track of unfinished work.

**Why this matters**
Helping a customer should not mean forgetting a half-completed task.

**Lesson content**

**Pause safely**
Put down tools and materials safely. Do not leave sharp objects, unstable stacks, or open cartons where customers could be hurt.

**Capture your place**
Use a permitted checklist or brief note to mark the next step. Never record unnecessary customer personal information.

**Return or hand off**
After helping, resume from the note. If the shift changes or a new priority takes over, tell the next person what remains.

**Hardware-store example**
While checking shelf labels, a customer asks for help finding a faucet connector. You make the area safe, mark the aisle section you were checking, and return after assisting.

**What to say or do**
Say: “I’m helping this customer. I’ve marked where I stopped so I can finish the label check.”

**Common mistakes**
Leaving trip hazards; relying on memory for many unfinished tasks; refusing reasonable customer help.

**When to ask for help**
Ask a coworker or manager when multiple customers need help or a safety-sensitive task cannot be paused.

### BL-103-L04 — Communicate When Priorities Change
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Objective:** Tell the right person when work must be rescheduled.

**Why this matters**
A silent change can leave important tasks unfinished.

**Lesson content**

**Give a short update**
State what was planned, what interrupted it, and what remains. Keep the update factual and concise.

**Ask for a decision**
If two tasks cannot both be completed, ask the responsible leader which has priority. Do not guess when a deadline or safety concern matters.

**Use teamwork**
Request a handoff or help when appropriate. Share enough context for the other person to continue without starting over.

**Hardware-store example**
A delivery arrives while you are setting a promotional display. You tell the manager the display is half finished and ask whether to help receive the truck.

**What to say or do**
Say: “The display needs about one more section. The truck is here. Which should I handle first?”

**Common mistakes**
Pretending both tasks are done; changing priorities without notice; blaming others for unclear handoffs.

**When to ask for help**
Escalate missed deadlines, customer commitments, or work requiring special authorization.

### BL-103-L05 — Close the Shift with a Useful Handoff
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Objective:** Leave clear information for the next employee.

**Why this matters**
Good handoffs prevent repeated work and missed customer promises.

**Lesson content**

**Record what is done**
Check off completed tasks and confirm any important results, such as corrected shelf labels or received cartons.

**Name what remains**
List unfinished tasks, location, urgency, and any relevant next step. Follow approved systems and privacy rules.

**Confirm ownership**
Tell the incoming employee or manager who will take the next action. Do not assume a note alone guarantees follow-through.

**Hardware-store example**
At shift change, you report that aisle four is stocked but two labels need printing, and you identify where the label request was saved.

**What to say or do**
Say: “Aisle four is stocked. Two labels are pending in the approved system, and the next shift has the request.”

**Common mistakes**
Saying only “not done”; leaving customer promises undocumented; sharing private details in open notes.

**When to ask for help**
Escalate urgent safety issues and time-sensitive customer commitments directly, not only in a written note.

**Optional practice — unscored**
BL-103-L05-P01: What belongs in a useful shift handoff?
- A. Only a general complaint
- B. Completed work, unfinished items, and who owns the next step
- C. Private customer details unrelated to the task
- D. Nothing if you are tired
**Answer:** B. Specific status and ownership help the next shift continue smoothly.
