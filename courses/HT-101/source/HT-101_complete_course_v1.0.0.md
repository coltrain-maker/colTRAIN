### HT-101-L01: Recognize Common Fasteners
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Distinguish nails, screws, bolts, nuts, and washers.

#### Why this matters
A customer asks for “a bolt” but holds a wood screw. Knowing the basic shapes helps you ask the right question.

#### Lesson content

**Five everyday fasteners**
Nails are driven into material, usually with a hammer or nailer. Screws have threads and are turned with a matching driver. Bolts commonly work with nuts or threaded holes. Nuts have internal threads. Washers can spread pressure or serve another purpose specified by the assembly.

**Name the parts**
On a screw or bolt, the head is the top. The shank is the body. Threads are the spiral ridges. A drive is the recess or shape used to turn the fastener. Common drives include slotted, Phillips, hex, and Torx.

**Match names to actual items**
Some customers use “screw” and “bolt” interchangeably. Show examples. Ask what the fastener joins and whether they have the old part or packaging.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer brings in a loose fastener from a patio chair. You identify its head and threads, then compare it with labeled samples instead of guessing its size.

#### What to say or do
- Ask what materials are being joined.
- Look at the original fastener if available.
- Identify the head, threads, and drive.
- Use labeled samples to narrow choices.

#### Common mistakes
- Calling every threaded fastener a bolt.
- Choosing by appearance alone.

#### When to ask for help
If a fastener is part of a structural, safety-critical, or manufacturer-specified assembly, consult the instructions or a qualified specialist.

### HT-101-L02: Read Fastener Sizes and Labels
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Explain diameter, length, thread, and package quantity.

#### Why this matters
A fastener that looks close may not fit. Labels help avoid damaged parts and wasted trips.

#### Lesson content

**Diameter and length**
Diameter describes how wide a fastener is. Length describes how long it is. Length conventions can vary by fastener head type, so check the product label or manufacturer diagram.

**Threads and units**
Thread pitch describes spacing between threads. Inch-series fasteners may show threads per inch. Metric fasteners often show pitch in millimeters. Do not mix thread systems because they look similar.

**Read the package**
Check the listed diameter, length, thread information, quantity, material, and any use limitations. Compare against the old fastener or product instructions when possible.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer needs a replacement machine screw. The old one looks similar to several options. You use a thread gauge or labeled sample board and confirm the marking before recommending a replacement.

#### What to say or do
- Read all size markings aloud.
- Compare the old part with a gauge if one is available.
- Check the unit system.
- Never force a test fit.

#### Common mistakes
- Guessing a thread from its appearance.
- Assuming length is measured the same way for every head.

#### When to ask for help
Refer uncertain thread identification, load requirements, and manufacturer assemblies to a trained employee or the product documentation.

### HT-101-L03: Choose Fasteners for the Material
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Ask material and environment questions before recommending a fastener.

#### Why this matters
Wood, metal, masonry, and drywall do not always use the same fasteners or anchors.

#### Lesson content

**Identify the base material**
Ask what material receives the fastener. Is it wood, sheet metal, masonry, drywall, or something else? Ask what is being attached and whether the attachment is indoors or outdoors.

**Understand anchors**
An anchor helps a fastener hold in a material when the fastener alone is not suitable. Anchors vary by base material and installation method. Never treat an anchor rating as universal.

**Check the environment**
Outdoor moisture and other exposure can affect fastener selection. Corrosion-resistant products exist, but suitability depends on the material combination and manufacturer instructions.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer wants to hang a heavy shelf on drywall. You ask whether the shelf can attach to framing and how much weight it must support. You avoid guessing at an anchor without verified ratings and instructions.

#### What to say or do
- Ask about the base material.
- Ask about weight and installation location.
- Check packaging for intended use.
- Escalate safety-critical installations.

#### Common mistakes
- Assuming a wood screw is suitable for masonry.
- Promising a load rating without checking conditions.

#### When to ask for help
Ask for help with overhead, structural, safety-critical, or heavy-load installations; follow manufacturer instructions and local requirements.

### HT-101-L04: Understand Heads, Drives, and Tools
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Match fastener heads and drives to suitable tools.

#### Why this matters
Using the wrong bit or wrench can strip a head or damage a project.

#### Lesson content

**Head shapes**
Common screw heads include flat or countersunk, pan, and hex. Head shape affects how the fastener sits and which tool engages it.

**Drive shapes**
A Phillips bit is different from a Torx bit. Hex-head fasteners may need a socket or wrench. Size matters as much as shape.

**Avoid damage**
A poorly fitting bit can slip. Encourage customers to check packaging and test fit the correct size without forcing it. Power tools require safe use according to instructions.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer says a screw head keeps stripping. You ask which bit they use and compare the drive marking on the screw package with available bits.

#### What to say or do
- Identify drive type and size.
- Check head clearance and access.
- Recommend the matching hand tool or bit when verified.
- Avoid promising a bit will fix an already damaged head.

#### Common mistakes
- Assuming all cross-shaped drives are the same.
- Using a loose-fitting bit.

#### When to ask for help
Escalate damaged fasteners, specialized drives, and safety-sensitive assemblies.

### HT-101-L05: Help a Customer Complete a Fastener Purchase
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Build a verified list without overselling.

#### Why this matters
Customers often need the right quantity and a matching tool, not just one fastener.

#### Lesson content

**Confirm the project**
Repeat the material, use, and fastener requirements. Check the customer’s original instructions when available.

**Check companion items**
Ask whether they have the matching driver, drill bit, nut, washer, or anchor required by the assembly. Do not add items that the customer does not need.

**Close with limits**
Confirm package quantity and measurements. Explain that product labels and instructions control proper installation. Escalate technical uncertainties.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer replacing a gate latch has the manufacturer instructions. You help locate the specified fastener size, confirm package quantity, and ask if they already have the correct driver.

#### What to say or do
- Confirm size, material, and quantity.
- Ask what tools the customer owns.
- Check product instructions.
- Summarize the selection before checkout.

#### Common mistakes
- Adding accessories without asking.
- Substituting a different fastener without verifying suitability.

#### When to ask for help
Get specialist help when required fasteners are unavailable or a substitution would change a rated assembly.
