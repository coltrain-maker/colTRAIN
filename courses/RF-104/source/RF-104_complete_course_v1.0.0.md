## 4. Individual lessons

### RF-104-L01 — Find Your Way Around the Store
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Identify common store areas and explain how they support customers.

#### Why this matters
Customers expect employees to help them find products. A clear picture of the store makes that easier.

#### Lesson content
**Common store areas**
Most hardware stores have a sales floor, checkout area, receiving area, stockroom, and service counter. Some stores also have a garden center or rental desk. Store layouts vary.

**The sales floor**
The sales floor is where customers shop. Products are often grouped by department. Aisles may include paint, plumbing, electrical, tools, hardware, and lawn and garden.

**Areas behind the scenes**
Receiving is where deliveries are checked. A stockroom holds merchandise that is not on the sales floor. Employees should enter restricted areas only with permission.

**Store maps and signs**
Aisle signs, shelf labels, and store maps can help you locate merchandise. Confirm the location when signs and inventory records do not agree.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer asks for weatherstripping. You know it may be near doors or insulation. You check the store directory and guide the customer to the correct area.

#### What to say or do
- Learn the names of your store’s main departments.
- Locate checkout, receiving, and customer service areas.
- Use approved store maps or coworkers to verify locations.

#### Common mistakes
- Assuming every hardware store uses the same layout.
- Sending a customer to an aisle without checking.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a coworker when a department or restricted area is unfamiliar.

#### Key takeaways
- Know the main store areas.

- Verify locations before directing customers.

#### Unscored lesson practice

**Explanation:** Store layouts vary. Verification is better than guessing.

### RF-104-L02 — Understand the Main Departments
**Estimated time:** 5 minutes
**Learning objective:** Recognize the purpose of common hardware-store departments.

#### Why this matters
Customers often describe a project instead of a product. Department knowledge helps you decide where to start.

#### Lesson content
**Hardware and fasteners**
This area often includes screws, bolts, nuts, washers, hinges, and other small parts. Size and material matter.

**Tools and power equipment**
Hand tools include hammers, wrenches, and screwdrivers. Power tools include drills and saws. Outdoor power equipment may have its own department.

**Home repair departments**
Plumbing often includes pipe, fittings, and repair parts. Electrical may include switches, outlets, and lighting products. Paint may include coatings, brushes, rollers, and preparation supplies.

**Lawn, garden, and building materials**
Lawn and garden may carry soil, seeds, fertilizers, and hoses. Building materials may include lumber, sheet goods, and concrete products. Not every store carries every category.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer wants to repair a dripping outdoor faucet. You recognize this as a plumbing question. You guide the customer to the plumbing area and help find an experienced associate.

#### What to say or do
- Learn two common product groups in each department.
- Ask customers what project they are completing.
- Refer detailed product questions to trained coworkers.

#### Common mistakes
- Assuming a product belongs in only one department.
- Confusing similar-looking parts.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a department specialist before recommending technical parts or products with safety requirements.

#### Supporting materials
- **Photo:** [Needed]. Placement: After the store areas or department overview. Purpose: Optional image of a representative store area. Accessibility: Provide an accurate image description when supplied. **Suggested only; no file supplied.**

#### Key takeaways
- Learn the purpose of each department.
- Listen for the customer’s project.
- Do not guess about technical product fit.

#### Unscored lesson practice
**Question (RF-104-L02-P01):** A customer asks for a roller cover and painter’s tape. Which department is a good starting point?
**Answer:** Paint and decorating.
**Explanation:** Both items are commonly used for painting projects.

### RF-104-L03 — Follow a Product Through the Store
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Describe the basic path of merchandise from delivery to sale.

#### Why this matters
Products must move through several steps before customers can buy them. Each step affects accuracy and availability.

#### Lesson content
**Receiving**
A delivery arrives at the store. Authorized employees check the shipment against the store’s receiving process. They report shortages or damage.

**Storage and stocking**
Products may go directly to shelves or into backstock. Backstock means extra merchandise kept off the sales floor. Products need accurate locations and labels.

**Selling and replenishment**
Customers buy products at checkout or through another approved sales channel. Sales and other inventory changes may update the store’s inventory records. The timing depends on the system.

**Returns and exceptions**
Returns, damaged goods, and special orders follow store-specific procedures. Do not put returned items back on shelves without following those procedures.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer wants a box of deck screws. The shelf is empty. The inventory record shows available stock. You ask an authorized coworker to check the backstock location before making a promise.

#### What to say or do
- Keep product labels with the correct merchandise.
- Report damage and quantity differences.
- Use the store’s approved receiving and return procedures.

#### Common mistakes
- Treating an inventory count as proof that an item is on the shelf.
- Putting unverified merchandise into saleable stock.

#### When to ask for help
Ask receiving or inventory staff when counts, locations, or product condition do not match.

#### Supporting materials
- No supporting media required. Text-only lesson.

#### Key takeaways
- Products move from receiving to storage or shelves.
- Accurate locations help customers.
- Exceptions need approved procedures.

#### Unscored lesson practice
**Question (RF-104-L03-P01):** The shelf is empty, but the system lists two items. Is the item definitely available?
**Answer:** No. Check the location and ask the appropriate employee to verify.
**Explanation:** Inventory records can differ from physical stock.

#### Optional practice
None required.

### RF-104-L04 — Know Common Store Services
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Identify common store services and explain how to verify availability.

#### Why this matters
Many customers visit hardware stores for services as well as products. Services differ by location.

#### Lesson content
**Services vary by store**
Some stores offer key cutting, paint mixing, screen repair, propane exchange, delivery, or equipment service. These are examples, not promises about every store.

**Ask before promising**
Services may require trained staff, special equipment, or scheduled hours. Confirm availability, pricing, and turnaround time through approved store resources.

**Understand your role**
You may greet the customer, gather basic details, or direct them to the correct counter. Only trained and authorized employees should operate service equipment.

**Set clear expectations**
Tell customers what has been confirmed. Avoid promising that a service can be completed immediately unless an authorized employee has verified it.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer asks whether the store can cut a replacement key today. You check the service list and ask the trained employee about availability. You then explain the next step.

#### What to say or do
- Learn which services your store actually offers.
- Find out who is authorized to perform each service.
- Verify hours, pricing, and timelines before making promises.

#### Common mistakes
- Assuming every location offers the same services.
- Operating service equipment without training.

#### When to ask for help
Ask the service lead or manager when availability, pricing, or authorization is unclear.

#### Supporting materials
- No supporting media required. Text-only lesson.

#### Key takeaways
- Services differ by store.
- Verify details before promising.
- Use trained staff for service equipment.

#### Unscored lesson practice
**Question (RF-104-L04-P01):** A customer asks for same-day screen repair. You do not know the schedule. What should you say?
**Answer:** Let me check whether we offer that service and what the current turnaround time is.
**Explanation:** Confirm service availability before setting expectations.

#### Optional practice
None required.

### RF-104-L05 — Connect Customers With the Right Help
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Choose the correct store resource or coworker for a customer request.

#### Why this matters
You do not need to know every product or service. You do need to help customers reach the right resource.

#### Lesson content
**Start with the need**
Listen to the customer’s project or request. Repeat the key detail in simple words to confirm your understanding.

**Choose a resource**
Use aisle signs for locations. Use approved inventory tools for stock checks. Use service lists for store services. Ask trained coworkers about specialized products.

**Make a useful handoff**
A handoff means passing a customer request to another employee. Share the important facts. Do not make the customer repeat the whole story when you can help.

**Follow through**
Confirm that the customer has reached the right person or next step. Do not leave a customer waiting without an update.

**Know when to stop**
Do not give instructions for licensed work or make unsupported safety claims. Refer technical questions to qualified people and approved manufacturer guidance.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer needs a part for a ceiling fan. You ask for the model information. You connect the customer with an associate who can check compatible replacement parts. You share the model details during the handoff.

#### What to say or do
- Listen and confirm the customer’s request.
- Choose the right department, resource, or trained coworker.
- Share useful details during a handoff.
- Confirm the next step with the customer.

#### Common mistakes
- Guessing a product location or fit.
- Pointing vaguely across the store.
- Leaving the customer without a clear next step.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a manager or trained coworker when the request involves technical compatibility, service promises, or safety.

#### Supporting materials
- No supporting media required. Text-only lesson.

#### Key takeaways
- Listen before directing.
- Use the right store resource.
- Give clear handoffs.
- Confirm the next step.

#### Unscored lesson practice
**Question (RF-104-L05-P01):** You cannot identify a replacement part. What information should you share with a specialist?
**Answer:** Share the customer’s project, the old part or model details, and what you have already checked.
**Explanation:** A clear handoff saves time and reduces confusion.
