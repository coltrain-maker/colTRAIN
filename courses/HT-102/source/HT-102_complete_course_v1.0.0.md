### HT-102-L01: Know the Main Hand Tool Families
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Recognize common hand tools and their purposes.

#### Why this matters
A customer may describe a job without knowing the name of the tool.

#### Lesson content

**Driving and turning**
Screwdrivers turn screws. Wrenches and sockets turn matching nuts or bolt heads. Pliers grip, bend, or cut within their intended use.

**Measuring and marking**
Tape measures, squares, and levels help check dimensions, angles, and alignment. Marking tools help transfer measurements to a workpiece.

**Cutting and striking**
Hand saws cut suitable materials. Utility knives cut materials allowed by the blade and tool instructions. Hammers strike nails or other items when designed for that purpose.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer asks for a tool to tighten a hex nut under a sink. You show a correctly sized wrench and ask about clearance before recommending one.

#### What to say or do
- Ask what the customer needs to do.
- Identify the fastener or material.
- Show two suitable tool categories when helpful.
- Explain each tool in everyday language.

#### Common mistakes
- Recommending a tool from its appearance alone.
- Using a cutting tool for a task it was not designed to perform.

#### When to ask for help
Ask for help with specialized tools, energized systems, and applications requiring professional qualifications.

### HT-102-L02: Choose the Right Size and Fit
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Match tools to fasteners, materials, and working space.

#### Why this matters
A correct tool type can still be wrong if it does not fit.

#### Lesson content

**Fit first**
Choose a screwdriver tip, socket, or wrench that matches the fastener type and size. Poor fit can round or strip parts.

**Consider access**
A deep socket, short handle, or different wrench shape may help in a tight area. Check the actual clearance before recommending a specific design.

**Choose by job**
A hand saw must be appropriate for the material. A measuring tool must offer the range and precision the customer needs. Verify tool specifications rather than assuming.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer needs to tighten a bolt behind a cabinet. You ask about the space around the bolt and the head size. You compare tool options without guaranteeing clearance.

#### What to say or do
- Confirm size and shape.
- Ask about working space.
- Check packaging for material and tool limits.
- Encourage a safe fit check.

#### Common mistakes
- Using adjustable tools carelessly on a poorly matched head.
- Choosing by handle color.

#### When to ask for help
Escalate if the customer cannot identify the fastener or if tool use could damage safety-critical equipment.

### HT-102-L03: Measure, Mark, and Check Work
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Explain basic measuring and layout tools.

#### Why this matters
A small measuring mistake can waste materials or make parts fail to fit.

#### Lesson content

**Measure carefully**
Start at the correct reference point. Read the unit marks and record the number. Measure again before cutting.

**Keep things straight**
A level checks orientation against level or plumb. A square helps check or mark right angles. Use each tool according to its design.

**Communicate measurements**
Repeat the number and unit. A measurement of 24 inches is not the same as 24 centimeters. Ask whether the measurement is exact or approximate.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer wants a shelf cut to size. You ask whether their measurement includes brackets and have them confirm the unit and finished dimension.

#### What to say or do
- Say the unit with every measurement.
- Check the reference edge.
- Measure twice before an irreversible cut.
- Verify store cutting policies separately.

#### Common mistakes
- Mixing units.
- Measuring from the wrong end of a damaged tape.

#### When to ask for help
Ask for help with precision layout, building-code questions, and store cutting services.

### HT-102-L04: Explain Basic Hand Tool Safety
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Recognize common risks and safe boundaries.

#### Why this matters
Even simple hand tools can injure users if damaged or misused.

#### Lesson content

**Inspect before use**
Look for cracked handles, loose heads, damaged cutting edges, and other defects. Do not use or sell a tool as safe when damage is suspected.

**Use tools as intended**
Wear protective equipment specified by the tool and task. Keep cutting edges away from the body. Secure workpieces when appropriate. Follow manufacturer instructions.

**Know what not to advise**
Do not suggest using ordinary hand tools on live electrical parts. Do not improvise a tool as a lever or striking device beyond its intended use.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer asks if a regular screwdriver can be used on an energized outlet. You do not advise working live. You direct them to a qualified electrician and appropriate safety guidance.

#### What to say or do
- Check tool condition.
- Explain the intended use.
- Refer to the manufacturer safety instructions.
- Stop when a task involves electrical or other serious hazards.

#### Common mistakes
- Treating all screwdrivers as safe for electrical work.
- Ignoring a loose hammer head.

#### When to ask for help
Refer electrical, gas, structural, and other high-risk tasks to qualified professionals.

### HT-102-L05: Care for and Store Hand Tools
**Time:** 5 minutes. **Objective:** Describe basic inspection, cleaning, and storage.

#### Why this matters
Well-kept tools are easier to find and less likely to fail unexpectedly.

#### Lesson content

**Clean and dry**
Remove ordinary dirt after use using manufacturer-approved methods. Dry tools before storage when appropriate. Follow instructions for rust prevention.

**Protect edges and moving parts**
Store sharp tools with guards when provided. Keep tools organized to prevent accidental contact and damage. Do not attempt repairs outside approved procedures.

**Know replacement signs**
Damaged handles, cracked sockets, or badly worn drivers may need replacement. Manufacturer guidance determines whether repair is allowed.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer asks how to store hand tools in a damp shed. You suggest a dry, organized location and checking the tool maker’s care guidance for rust prevention.

#### What to say or do
- Recommend a dry storage space.
- Keep sharp edges protected.
- Inspect tools before use.
- Refer damaged tools to appropriate service guidance.

#### Common mistakes
- Leaving tools wet in a closed container.
- Continuing to use a cracked tool.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a supervisor or manufacturer service contact about uncertain repairs or warranty questions.
