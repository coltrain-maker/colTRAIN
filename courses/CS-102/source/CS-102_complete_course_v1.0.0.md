## 1. Course information
- **Course code:** CS-102
- **Course title:** Selling Skills for Hardware Retail
- **Short description:** Learn how to understand a customer’s project, compare suitable products, and suggest useful supplies without pressure.

## 4. Individual lessons

### Lesson 1: Discover the Customer’s Project (CS-102-L01)
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Ask questions that uncover the customer’s goal and project requirements.

#### Why this matters
A customer may name a product before explaining the job. A few questions can prevent a poor match.

#### Lesson content

**Start with the project**

Ask what the customer wants to accomplish. Learn whether this is a repair, replacement, or new project. Ask what they have already tried.

**Collect the useful details**

Ask about the material, size, location, and conditions. Ask whether the customer has a photo, old part, or instructions. Only collect details that matter to the choice.

**Confirm what you heard**

Repeat the main need in plain language. For example: “You need a way to fasten this gate latch to a wood post. Is that right?”

#### Hardware-store example
A customer asks for screws. You ask what they are fastening. They say a gate latch to a wooden post outdoors. You now know the job and the location. You still need the latch instructions and size details before suggesting a fastener.

#### What to say or do
- Ask: “What are you working on?”
- Ask: “Where will you use it?”
- Ask: “Do you have the old part or instructions?”
- Repeat the project goal before moving to products.

#### Common mistakes
- Starting with the most expensive product.
- Assuming a familiar item fits every project.
- Asking many questions without explaining why.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a knowledgeable coworker when the customer cannot describe the job clearly. Refer to product instructions for load, electrical, structural, or other safety-sensitive applications.

#### Supporting materials
- **Actual media:** None.
- **Suggested media:** None required. These lessons use text scenarios. No stock photo is included because a generic photo would not clarify the decisions being taught.
- **Accessibility:** All teaching content is available as readable text.

#### Key takeaways
- Start with the customer’s goal.
- Ask only useful questions.
- Confirm the project before choosing products.

#### Unscored lesson practice
**Practice ID:** CS-102-P01
**Question:** A customer asks for caulk. What should you ask first?
- **A.** “Which brand costs the most?”
- **B.** “What surface or gap are you sealing?”
- **C.** “Do you want two tubes?”
- **D.** “Can you return tomorrow?”
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The surface and location help narrow the correct product. Price and quantity come later.

### Lesson 2: Match Features to Customer Needs (CS-102-L02)
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Explain product differences using the customer’s project needs.

#### Why this matters
Customers do not always know why two similar products have different prices or uses.

#### Lesson content

**Features and benefits**

A feature is a product fact. A benefit explains why that fact matters to the customer. For example, a longer hose is a feature. Reaching a distant garden bed is the benefit.

**Compare only relevant details**

Use the project to select comparison points. These may include dimensions, material, capacity, compatibility, maintenance, and price. Verify product claims on packaging or official instructions.

**Be clear about limits**

Do not promise that a product will work in every situation. If you cannot confirm compatibility, say what you need to check.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer needs a hose for a garden bed far from the spigot. You compare two hose lengths. You explain the reach of each option. You also ask about storage and the path to the garden.

#### What to say or do
- Say: “This option may fit your project because…”
- Point to a verified feature on the label.
- Explain a benefit in everyday words.
- Mention an important limitation.

#### Common mistakes
- Listing features without explaining them.
- Calling one product “best” for everyone.
- Making claims that are not on verified product information.

#### When to ask for help
Ask for help when a product rating, compatibility requirement, warranty, or safety instruction is unclear. Use current manufacturer documentation.

#### Supporting materials
- **Actual media:** None.
- **Suggested media:** None required. These lessons use text scenarios. No stock photo is included because a generic photo would not clarify the decisions being taught.
- **Accessibility:** All teaching content is available as readable text.

#### Key takeaways
- Explain benefits, not just features.
- Compare products using the project.
- Verify claims before sharing them.

#### Unscored lesson practice
**Practice ID:** CS-102-P02
**Question:** A customer needs a hose to reach a distant garden bed. Which statement is most helpful?
- **A.** “This hose is popular.”
- **B.** “This hose is the most expensive.”
- **C.** “This longer hose may reach the bed. Let’s confirm the distance.”
- **D.** “All hoses work the same.”
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** This answer connects a feature to the customer’s need. It also checks a missing detail.

### Lesson 3: Build a Complete Project List (CS-102-L03)
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Identify useful companion items without pressuring the customer.

#### Why this matters
A customer may get home and discover a missing supply. Helpful selling reduces extra trips.

#### Lesson content

**Think through the job**

Ask what tools and materials the customer already has. Review the main steps of the project. Check instructions for required accessories and preparation.

**Offer relevant items**

A paint project may also need tape, a drop cloth, a tray, and a suitable roller. These are examples, not a fixed shopping list. Match items to the surface and product instructions.

**Respect the customer’s choice**

Explain why each item may help. Let the customer decide. Do not add products without permission.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer buys paint for a bedroom. You ask whether they have a roller, tray, and supplies to protect the floor. They have a roller but need a tray. You show an appropriate option and explain its use.

#### What to say or do
- Ask: “What supplies do you already have?”
- Say: “You may also need this for the next step.”
- Explain the reason for each suggestion.
- Accept a “no” without pressure.

#### Common mistakes
- Adding items to raise the sale total.
- Assuming every customer needs a full kit.
- Forgetting to check product instructions.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a department specialist about product compatibility or application requirements. Do not give unverified safety or installation advice.

#### Supporting materials
- **Actual media:** None.
- **Suggested media:** None required. These lessons use text scenarios. No stock photo is included because a generic photo would not clarify the decisions being taught.
- **Accessibility:** All teaching content is available as readable text.

#### Key takeaways
- Help customers avoid missing supplies.
- Explain each companion item.
- Respect customer decisions.

### Lesson 4: Discuss Price and Value Clearly (CS-102-L04)
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Compare options and respond to price concerns without pressure.

#### Why this matters
Customers often weigh price against durability, convenience, and project needs.

#### Lesson content

**Understand the budget**

Ask whether the customer has a price range. Do not assume the least expensive option is the best fit.

**Compare total value**

Discuss verified differences that matter to the project. A lower price may be important. So may product life, quantity, or required accessories. Avoid unsupported claims about savings.

**Respond without arguing**

Acknowledge the concern. Offer a clear comparison. Give the customer room to choose.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer says a drill costs more than expected. You ask how often they plan to use it. You compare two suitable models using verified specifications. You explain the differences and let them choose.

#### What to say or do
- Say: “What price range works for you?”
- Say: “Here are the differences that matter for your project.”
- Offer a suitable lower-cost option when available.
- Do not criticize a customer’s budget.

#### Common mistakes
- Treating a price concern as an objection to defeat.
- Promising savings without evidence.
- Assuming expensive always means better.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a manager about promotions, price adjustments, financing, or return policies. Never invent store terms or discounts.

#### Supporting materials
- **Actual media:** None.
- **Suggested media:** None required. These lessons use text scenarios. No stock photo is included because a generic photo would not clarify the decisions being taught.
- **Accessibility:** All teaching content is available as readable text.

#### Key takeaways
- Listen to price concerns.
- Compare verified value.
- Let the customer decide.

#### Unscored lesson practice
**Practice ID:** CS-102-P04
**Question:** A customer says a drill is too expensive. What should you do?

- **C.** Ask about their use and budget. Compare suitable options.
- **D.** End the conversation.
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The goal is a suitable choice within the customer’s needs and budget.

### Lesson 5: Close the Sale with Confidence and Care (CS-102-L05)
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Confirm the choice and end the interaction with a clear next step.

#### Why this matters
A good sale ends with a customer who understands the product and what happens next.

#### Lesson content

**Confirm the choice**

Summarize the product and the project need. Ask whether the customer has remaining questions.

**Check practical details**

Confirm quantity, size, color, or compatibility as appropriate. Mention product instructions and important use limits. Do not invent return or warranty rules.

**End with a clear handoff**

Direct the customer to checkout or the right service desk. Thank them. If a specialist must finish the consultation, introduce the customer and share what you learned.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer selects a garden hose. You confirm the length and connector type shown on the package. You ask if they need anything else. Then you guide them toward checkout.

#### What to say or do
- Say: “Does this meet what you need for the project?”
- Confirm key details before checkout.
- Ask: “Is there anything else you need?”
- Thank the customer without pressure.

#### Common mistakes
- Rushing the customer to checkout.
- Making a final promise about unverified compatibility.
- Leaving the customer without a next step.

#### When to ask for help
Ask for help with unresolved product questions, unusual orders, or store policy. For high-risk applications, refer to manufacturer guidance or a qualified professional.

#### Supporting materials
- **Actual media:** None.
- **Suggested media:** None required. These lessons use text scenarios. No stock photo is included because a generic photo would not clarify the decisions being taught.
- **Accessibility:** All teaching content is available as readable text.

#### Key takeaways
- Confirm the product and need.
- Check important details.
- Close with respect and clarity.

#### Unscored lesson practice
**Practice ID:** CS-102-P05
**Question:** A customer has chosen a replacement part. What is the best way to finish?
- **A.** Promise it will fit without checking.
- **B.** Confirm key details and ask about remaining questions.
- **C.** Add an unrelated item to the purchase.
- **D.** Leave without a handoff.
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A final check can catch missing details and gives the customer a clear next step.
