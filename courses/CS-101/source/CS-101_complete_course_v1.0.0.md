## 4. Individual lessons

### CS-101-L01 — Make Every Customer Feel Welcome
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes  
**Learning objective:** Greet customers and offer help without pressure.

#### Why this matters
Customers may feel unsure when they enter a hardware store. A friendly greeting helps them feel comfortable. It also shows that help is available.

#### Lesson content

**Start with a greeting**
Look up when a customer approaches. Use a friendly tone. Say hello and offer help. Give the customer space if they prefer to browse.

**Respect different shopping styles**
Some customers know the exact item they need. Others need time to think. Do not assume that every customer wants a long conversation.

**Stay available**
A customer may decline help at first. Let them know where to find you. Check back when it makes sense. Do not follow them around the store.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer enters and looks at the aisle signs. You say, “Welcome! Can I help you find something?” The customer says they want to look around. You reply, “Of course. I’ll be near the front if you need anything.”

#### What to say or do
- Greet customers in a friendly voice.
- Offer help with a simple question.
- Respect a customer who wants to browse.
- Stay easy to find.

#### Common mistakes
- Ignoring a customer because you are stocking shelves.
- Asking too many questions after a customer declines help.
- Assuming a customer knows where products are located.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a coworker or manager if you cannot leave an assigned station or if customers need help in another area.

### CS-101-L02 — Listen Before You Recommend
**Estimated time:** 5 minutes  
**Learning objective:** Use questions and listening to understand a customer’s goal.

#### Why this matters
Customers often describe a problem instead of naming a product. Good questions help employees understand the job before suggesting an item.

#### Lesson content

**Learn the goal**
Start with the project. Ask, “What are you working on?” Let the customer finish their explanation.

**Ask one useful question at a time**
Ask about the material, location, or existing part. Use words the customer understands. Avoid a long list of questions.

**Repeat the need**
Summarize what you heard. You might say, “You need a way to hang a picture on a plaster wall. Is that right?” This gives the customer a chance to correct you.

**Avoid guessing**
Similar products may serve different purposes. Check labels and instructions. Ask for help when product suitability is uncertain.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer asks for “the glue for outside.” You ask what they are joining and where the repair will be used. The customer says they need to repair a wooden planter. You check product labels before suggesting options.

#### What to say or do
- Ask about the project first.
- Listen without interrupting.
- Confirm your understanding.
- Verify product details before recommending an item.

#### Common mistakes
- Recommending the first familiar product.
- Using technical words without explaining them.
- Treating a customer’s first description as complete.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a knowledgeable coworker or refer to official product instructions when the application is unclear. Refer specialized or regulated work to a qualified professional.

### CS-101-L03 — Give Clear and Honest Help
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes  
**Learning objective:** Explain next steps clearly and handle unknown answers with honesty.

#### Why this matters
Customers need information they can use. Clear words and honest answers build confidence. Guessing can cause mistakes and frustration.

#### Lesson content

**Use everyday language**
Name the aisle or product group. Point out a label or feature. Explain unfamiliar terms in simple words.

**Be honest about limits**
You do not need to know every answer. Say, “I want to check that before I recommend it.” Then use an approved resource or ask a coworker.

**Make a useful handoff**
Explain the customer’s question to the next employee. Stay with the customer when practical. Do not send them from person to person without context.

**Avoid promises you cannot confirm**
Check inventory, service availability, and delivery details before making a promise. Store systems and procedures vary.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer wants to know whether a particular light bulb works with a dimmer. You check the package. The label is unclear. You say, “Let me verify that before you buy it.” You ask a trained coworker to help.

#### What to say or do
- Use short, clear explanations.
- Show where to find product information.
- Say when you need to verify an answer.
- Explain the customer’s need during a handoff.

#### Common mistakes
- Guessing to sound confident.
- Giving directions that are too vague.
- Promising a product or service without checking.

#### When to ask for help
Consult product labels, manufacturer instructions, an experienced coworker, or a manager. Do not give instructions for work that requires a qualified professional.

### CS-101-L04 — Handle Frustration with Respect
**Estimated time:** 5 minutes  
**Learning objective:** Respond calmly to a disappointed customer and know when to involve a manager.

#### Why this matters
Customers sometimes face delays, missing products, or confusing information. A calm response can keep a problem from getting worse.

#### Lesson content

**Listen first**
Let the customer explain the concern. Keep your voice steady. Avoid interrupting or arguing.

**Acknowledge the problem**
Show that you understand the inconvenience. You might say, “I can see why that is frustrating.” Do not promise a result before you know what is possible.

**Offer a clear next step**
Check the facts. Explain the options allowed by store policy. Ask a manager to help with decisions outside your authority.

**Protect everyone’s safety**
Do not accept threats or abusive behavior as part of the job. Follow store procedures. Get a manager or emergency help when needed.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer says a special-order item has not arrived. You listen and acknowledge the delay. You check the order status through the approved process. You explain what you can confirm and ask a manager to assist with options.

#### What to say or do
- Stay calm and listen.
- Acknowledge the concern without blame.
- Check facts before offering a solution.
- Escalate issues beyond your authority.

#### Common mistakes
- Arguing about who caused the problem.
- Promising a refund or delivery date without approval.
- Taking an angry comment personally.

#### When to ask for help
Involve a manager for policy exceptions, refund disputes, repeated complaints, threats, or unsafe behavior. Follow the store’s incident and emergency procedures.

### CS-101-L05 — Finish the Interaction Well
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes  
**Learning objective:** Confirm next steps and close a customer interaction professionally.

#### Why this matters
A helpful conversation can still end poorly if the customer does not know what happens next. A clear close reduces confusion.

#### Lesson content

**Confirm the result**
Ask whether the customer found what they need. If another person will help, explain who and where.

**Be clear about follow-up**
For orders or services, share only verified details. Explain how the customer will receive updates under the store’s process.

**Offer one final opportunity**
Ask, “Is there anything else I can help you find?” Do not pressure the customer to buy more.

**Thank the customer**
End with a friendly thank-you. A simple close shows respect for their time.

#### Hardware-store example
You help a customer locate a paint roller. They also need a tray. You show them the tray options. You ask whether they need anything else. The customer says no. You thank them and point out the checkout area.

#### What to say or do
- Confirm the customer’s next step.
- Check whether more help is needed.
- Avoid unverified promises.
- Thank the customer.

#### Common mistakes
- Leaving without saying what happens next.
- Assuming the customer has everything they need.
- Using a sales pitch when the customer is ready to leave.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a coworker or manager when an order, service, or complaint still needs follow-up. Confirm who owns the next step.
