# CS-103: Helping Customers When You Don't Know the Answer

## 1. Course information
- **Course code:** CS-103
- **Course title:** Helping Customers When You Don't Know the Answer
- **Short description:** Learn how to respond with confidence when a customer asks an unfamiliar question. Gather details, verify information, and make a helpful handoff.
- **Category:** Academy 2 — Customer Experience & Sales
- **Skill level:** Foundations (Level 1)
- **Intended audience:** New hires and experienced employees learning or reviewing core retail skills.
- **Relevant departments:** All sales and service departments.
- **Scope:** General colTRAIN course.
- **Store restrictions:** None in the content. Availability is controlled by the platform administrator.
- **Estimated completion time:** 25–30 minutes (five lessons at about four minutes each; quiz about five to ten minutes). Not yet employee-tested.
- **Prerequisites:** None required. RF-101 and CS-101 are useful prior learning.
- **Learning objectives:**
  1. Respond professionally when you do not know an answer.
  2. Gather the information needed to research a customer question.
  3. Check the best available source before giving technical advice.
  4. Connect the customer with the right person and share what you learned.
  5. Recognize questions that need a manager, manufacturer, or qualified professional.

## 4. Individual lessons

### Lesson 1: Stay Calm and Take Ownership (CS-103-L01)
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Respond professionally when you do not know an answer.

#### Why this matters
Customers expect help. They do not expect every new employee to know everything.

#### Lesson content

**Be honest and helpful**

Say you want to verify the answer. Keep a calm tone. Avoid guessing or dismissing the customer.

**Own the next step**

Explain what you will do next. You might check a label, consult a coworker, or find official instructions.

**Stay connected**

Do not send a customer away without a clear handoff. If you must leave, explain where you are going and return as promised.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer asks whether a certain blade fits their saw. You are unsure. You say you will check the saw model and blade information before giving an answer.

#### What to say or do
- Say: “I want to make sure we get this right.”
- Ask for the product model or package.
- Explain the next step.
- Return with an answer or a clear referral.

#### Common mistakes
- Guessing to sound confident.
- Saying “I don’t know” and walking away.
- Promising an answer you cannot verify.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a specialist when product compatibility or safe use is uncertain. Never recommend an unverified blade or accessory.

#### Supporting materials
- **Actual media:** None.
- **Suggested media:** None required. These lessons use text scenarios. No stock photo is included because a generic photo would not clarify the decisions being taught.
- **Accessibility:** All teaching content is available as readable text.

#### Key takeaways
- Be honest.
- Take responsibility for the next step.
- Keep the customer informed.

#### Unscored lesson practice
**Practice ID:** CS-103-P01
**Question:** You do not know whether a blade fits a saw. What should you say?
- **A.** “It probably fits.”
- **B.** “I’m new. Ask someone else.”
- **C.** “Let’s check the model and the blade specifications.”
- **D.** “All blades fit most saws.”
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Checking the model and specifications is a useful next step. It avoids guessing.

### Lesson 2: Ask Questions That Find the Missing Detail (CS-103-L02)
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Gather the information needed to research a customer question.

#### Why this matters
Many questions are hard to answer because key details are missing.

#### Lesson content

**Clarify the problem**

Ask what the customer is trying to do. Find out what is not working or what needs replacing.

**Identify the product**

Ask for a model number, measurements, old part, package, or photo. Do not assume similar-looking parts are interchangeable.

**Repeat the request**

Summarize what you know. This helps the customer correct missing or misunderstood details.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer needs a replacement filter but does not know the size. You ask for the equipment model and the label on the old filter. You can now look for a verified match.

#### What to say or do
- Ask: “What equipment is this for?”
- Ask: “Do you have the model number?”
- Ask: “Can you show me the old part or a photo?”
- Repeat the key details.

#### Common mistakes
- Searching without enough information.
- Assuming size from appearance.
- Asking for details unrelated to the problem.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a coworker for help identifying a model or part. Use manufacturer documentation for compatibility.

#### Supporting materials
- **Actual media:** None.
- **Suggested media:** None required. These lessons use text scenarios. No stock photo is included because a generic photo would not clarify the decisions being taught.
- **Accessibility:** All teaching content is available as readable text.

#### Key takeaways
- Find the missing detail.
- Use product identifiers.
- Confirm what you heard.

#### Unscored lesson practice

### Lesson 3: Use Reliable Information (CS-103-L03)
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Check the best available source before giving technical advice.

#### Why this matters
A confident guess can lead to a wrong purchase or unsafe use.

#### Lesson content

**Start with the product**

Read the package and labels. Look for model numbers, sizes, ratings, and warnings. Do not treat packaging as a substitute for full instructions when more detail is needed.

**Use official instructions**

For compatibility, safety, and warranty questions, prefer current manufacturer information. Check that the document matches the exact model.

**Know what is not confirmed**

If you cannot verify a claim, tell the customer. Explain the next safe step.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer asks whether a particular battery works with a power tool. You compare the exact tool and battery models using official compatibility information. You do not rely on color or appearance.

#### What to say or do
- Say: “Let’s check the model information.”
- Read the label with the customer.
- Use current official product instructions when available.
- State clearly what remains unverified.

#### Common mistakes
- Relying on memory for a safety claim.
- Using instructions for a different model.
- Treating an online comment as a manufacturer policy.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a specialist or manager if official information is unavailable or unclear. Refer the customer to manufacturer support or a qualified professional when appropriate.

#### Supporting materials
- **Actual media:** None.
- **Suggested media:** None required. These lessons use text scenarios. No stock photo is included because a generic photo would not clarify the decisions being taught.
- **Accessibility:** All teaching content is available as readable text.

#### Key takeaways
- Check exact models.
- Prefer official instructions.
- Do not present guesses as facts.

#### Unscored lesson practice

**Answer:** C

### Lesson 4: Make a Helpful Handoff (CS-103-L04)
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Connect the customer with the right person and share what you learned.

#### Why this matters
A customer should not need to repeat the same story several times.

#### Lesson content

**Choose the right helper**

Find a coworker with relevant product knowledge. Use a manager for store policy or an unresolved service issue.

**Summarize the need**

Tell the next employee what the customer wants, what details you checked, and what remains unanswered.

**Follow through**

Confirm that the handoff happened. If no one is available, explain the options without promising a time you cannot guarantee.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer needs a part for a pressure washer. You have the model number but cannot confirm the fitting. You introduce the customer to the power equipment specialist and share the model number and the question.

#### What to say or do
- Say: “I have the model number. Let me bring in our equipment specialist.”
- Share the details already collected.
- Confirm the customer knows who will help next.
- Avoid promising a wait time without checking.

#### Common mistakes
- Pointing vaguely across the store.
- Making the customer repeat every detail.
- Leaving before confirming the handoff.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a manager when a specialist is unavailable or a customer needs a policy decision. Refer technical work beyond retail advice to a qualified professional.

#### Supporting materials
- **Actual media:** None.
- **Suggested media:** None required. These lessons use text scenarios. No stock photo is included because a generic photo would not clarify the decisions being taught.

### Lesson 5: Know When to Stop and Escalate (CS-103-L05)
**Estimated time:** 4 minutes
**Learning objective:** Recognize questions that need a manager, manufacturer, or qualified professional.

#### Why this matters
Some questions involve risks or decisions outside a retail employee’s authority.

#### Lesson content

**Recognize higher-risk topics**

Electrical wiring, gas connections, structural loads, chemical mixing, and power equipment safety can involve serious hazards. Product advice is not a substitute for professional work.

**Separate product facts from professional advice**

You may help locate a product or find official instructions. Do not design, approve, or certify work that requires a qualified professional.

**Escalate clearly**

Explain why you need another source. Ask a manager for store policies. Use official manufacturer support for unresolved product questions. Recommend a qualified professional for work beyond your role.

#### Hardware-store example
A customer asks you to confirm that a fastener will support a heavy overhead load. You can help find product specifications. You do not approve the installation. You explain that the load and installation need qualified evaluation.

#### What to say or do
- Say: “I can help find the product specifications.”
- Say: “I cannot confirm this installation is safe.”
- Refer to a qualified professional for technical approval.
- Ask a manager about store policy.

#### Common mistakes
- Treating a package rating as approval for every installation.
- Giving installation advice outside your training.
- Ignoring a warning because a customer wants a quick answer.

#### When to ask for help
Escalate immediately when there is a safety concern, an unclear manufacturer warning, or a request for technical approval outside retail scope.

#### Supporting materials
- **Actual media:** None.
- **Suggested media:** None required. These lessons use text scenarios. No stock photo is included because a generic photo would not clarify the decisions being taught.
- **Accessibility:** All teaching content is available as readable text.

#### Key takeaways
- Know your role limits.
- Escalate safety-sensitive questions.
- Give a clear next step.

#### Unscored lesson practice
**Practice ID:** CS-103-P05
**Question:** A customer asks you to approve hardware for a heavy overhead installation. What should you do?
- **A.** Guarantee the hardware is safe.
- **B.** Recommend the biggest fastener.
- **C.** Find product specifications and refer the installation decision to a qualified professional.
- **D.** Tell the customer any fastener will work.
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Retail employees can provide verified product facts. They should not certify a high-risk installation.
