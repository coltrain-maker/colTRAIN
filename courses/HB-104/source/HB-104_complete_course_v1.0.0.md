### HB-104-L01: Recognize Lumber and Sheet Goods (5 minutes)

**Objective:** Identify common wood product categories

**Why this matters:** Customers need help locating the correct material before comparing options.

#### Know the basics
Dimensional lumber, plywood, OSB, MDF, and treated lumber are common categories. Plywood uses bonded layers of wood veneer. OSB uses bonded wood strands. MDF is made from wood fibers and resin.

#### Check before recommending
Products differ in intended use, finish, moisture resistance, and structural ratings. A sheet that looks similar may not have the same approved application.

#### Put it into practice
Use shelf tags and grade stamps or manufacturer labels to identify products. Do not describe all sheet goods as interchangeable.

**Hardware-store example:** A customer asks for “a four-by-eight board.” Ask whether they mean plywood, OSB, or another sheet material and what the project requires.

**What to say or do:** Ask: “Will this be indoors or outdoors? Is it decorative or part of a structure?”

**Common mistakes:** Assuming every 4-by-8 sheet has the same strength or moisture rating.

**When to ask for help:** Refer structural and code-related selections to qualified guidance.

**Key takeaways:**
- Dimensional lumber, plywood, OSB, MDF, and treated lumber are common categories.
- Products differ in intended use, finish, moisture resistance, and structural ratings.
- Use shelf tags and grade stamps or manufacturer labels to identify products.

### HB-104-L02: Understand Nominal and Actual Lumber Sizes (5 minutes)

**Objective:** Explain how lumber dimensions are labeled

**Why this matters:** A lumber size on a shelf tag may differ from its measured dimensions.

#### Know the basics
Dimensional lumber often uses nominal names such as 2×4. A typical surfaced 2×4 has actual dimensions of about 1.5 by 3.5 inches, but product standards and local stock must be checked.

#### Check before recommending
Length is another dimension and should be confirmed separately. Sheet goods also have thickness and size labels. Do not confuse nominal thickness with actual thickness.

#### Put it into practice
Customers may need exact finished dimensions for cabinetry or other close-fitting projects. Encourage measuring and checking the actual product.

**Hardware-store example:** A customer wants a board exactly two inches thick. Explain that a typical surfaced 2×4 is not two inches thick and help check the actual size.

**What to say or do:** Say: “That is the nominal size. Let’s verify the actual dimensions for your project.”

**Common mistakes:** Promising a nominal 2×4 measures exactly 2 by 4 inches.

**When to ask for help:** Escalate precision, engineered materials, and structural sizing questions.

**Key takeaways:**

- Length is another dimension and should be confirmed separately.
- Customers may need exact finished dimensions for cabinetry or other close-fitting projects.

### HB-104-L03: Match Material to Environment (5 minutes)

**Objective:** Recognize exposure and treatment labels

**Why this matters:** Moisture and ground contact can change material requirements.

#### Know the basics
Pressure-treated lumber is made for specific exposure conditions. Not all treated boards are rated for ground contact. The treatment label describes permitted uses and may identify fastener compatibility needs.

#### Check before recommending
Exterior-rated and interior-only sheet goods also differ. Paint or a protective coating does not automatically make an interior-only material suitable outdoors.

#### Put it into practice
Ask whether the material will touch soil, be exposed to rain, or remain dry. Confirm product markings and applicable building requirements.

**Hardware-store example:** A customer needs lumber for a raised garden bed. Ask about soil contact and read the treatment label rather than assuming every treated board is the same.

**What to say or do:** Say: “Let’s check whether this material is labeled for the exposure you described.”

**Common mistakes:** Assuming any pressure-treated board is ground-contact rated.

**When to ask for help:** Escalate structural outdoor projects, unusual exposure, and compatibility with fasteners.

**Supporting material:** Comparison of treatment labels: above-ground and ground-contact uses — **[Needed; not embedded]**. Placement: after lesson content. Alt text draft: Comparison of treatment labels: above-ground and ground-contact uses. Licensed image/technical verification required.

**Key takeaways:**
- Pressure-treated lumber is made for specific exposure conditions.
- Exterior-rated and interior-only sheet goods also differ.
- Ask whether the material will touch soil, be exposed to rain, or remain dry.

### HB-104-L04: Identify Basic Building Materials (5 minutes)

**Objective:** Recognize common project material families

**Why this matters:** A complete project may involve more than lumber.

#### Know the basics
Building material aisles may include drywall, cement board, insulation, masonry products, adhesives, and sealants. Each category has a different purpose.

#### Check before recommending
Drywall is commonly used for interior wall surfaces. Cement board may serve as a substrate in specified tile assemblies. Insulation products vary by type and application.

#### Put it into practice
Manufacturer instructions and local codes govern installation and suitability. Do not substitute one material for another based only on appearance or price.

**Hardware-store example:** A customer asks for a board behind shower tile. Ask for the planned assembly and direct them to the approved system instructions rather than recommending ordinary drywall.

**What to say or do:** Say: “The wall system matters. Let’s check the installation requirements for that application.”

**Common mistakes:** Treating drywall and cement board as equivalent in wet areas.

**When to ask for help:** Escalate fire ratings, waterproofing, insulation requirements, and structural assemblies.

**Key takeaways:**
- Building material aisles may include drywall, cement board, insulation, masonry products, adhesives, and sealants.
- Drywall is commonly used for interior wall surfaces.
- Manufacturer instructions and local codes govern installation and suitability.

### HB-104-L05: Help Customers Plan a Safe Materials Purchase (5 minutes)

**Objective:** Collect dimensions without designing structures

**Why this matters:** Good questions prevent wasted trips and unsafe substitutions.

#### Know the basics
Ask for project purpose, dimensions, exposure, finish requirements, and any plans or material lists. Distinguish rough estimates from verified specifications.

#### Check before recommending
You can help count items on a customer-provided list and identify labeled products. Do not invent beam spans, load capacities, structural designs, or building-code approvals.

#### Put it into practice
Large sheet goods and long boards may require safe handling and transport. Follow store procedures for lifting, cutting services, and vehicle loading.

**Hardware-store example:** A customer building a deck asks which joist size is safe. Explain that structural sizing depends on loads, spans, materials, and code. Offer to help locate items from a verified plan.

**What to say or do:** Say: “I can help find materials from your approved list. Structural sizing should be confirmed by a qualified professional.”

**Common mistakes:** Guessing structural member sizes based on another customer’s project.

**When to ask for help:** Escalate load-bearing design, cutting equipment, and unsafe loading situations.

**Supporting material:** Illustration of measuring a sheet and checking a customer material list — **[Needed; not embedded]**. Placement: after lesson content. Alt text draft: Illustration of measuring a sheet and checking a customer material list. Licensed image/technical verification required.

**Key takeaways:**
- Ask for project purpose, dimensions, exposure, finish requirements, and any plans or material lists.
- You can help count items on a customer-provided list and identify labeled products.
- Large sheet goods and long boards may require safe handling and transport.

**Unscored practice HB-104-P05:** What is the safest response to a request for an unverified deck joist size?
- A. Guess based on common stock
- B. Use the cheapest board
- C. Request qualified structural guidance and help with a verified list
- D. Promise any treated board will work
**Answer:** C. **Explanation:** Structural sizing requires verified design and code considerations.
