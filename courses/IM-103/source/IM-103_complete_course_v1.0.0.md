### IM-103-L01: See the Store Through the Customer’s Eyes
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Objective:** Understand how product presentation affects shopping.

#### Why this matters
Merchandising is the way a store organizes and presents products for customers. Good merchandising helps a shopper find the right item, compare choices, and understand what else a project may require.

#### Lesson content
**Make products easy to find.** Clear categories, readable signs, and organized shelves reduce confusion. Customers should not need to guess where related items are kept.

**Help customers compare.** Group products according to the store’s approved plan. Make differences such as size, use, and package count visible.

**Keep information accurate.** A tidy display is not helpful if the shelf label belongs to another product.

#### Hardware-store example
A shopper needs a paint roller. The associate points to a section where roller covers are arranged by size and type, with clear labels.

#### What to say or do
- Walk an aisle as a first-time shopper would.
- Check whether categories and labels make sense.
- Report confusing signs or mixed products.

#### Common mistakes
- Assuming customers know store terminology.
- Using an attractive sign that is hard to read.
- Ignoring misplaced products.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a department lead before changing the store’s layout or category grouping.

#### Key takeaways

- D. Reduce employee conversations.
**Answer:** B. Merchandising supports clear, confident shopping.

### IM-103-L02: Match Products, Locations, and Shelf Labels
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Objective:** Keep merchandise and price information aligned.

#### Why this matters
Shelf tags and signs should identify the product actually displayed. Similar packages can vary in size, quantity, finish, or price. Always check the item identifier rather than guessing from appearance.

#### Lesson content
**Read the details.** Confirm SKU or barcode, description, size, unit, and price display according to store policy. If the label seems wrong, request verification before replacing it.

**Follow the approved plan.** A planogram is a diagram or guide showing where products should be placed. Not every store uses one. Follow the store’s approved shelf arrangement or manager direction.

**Avoid misleading displays.** Do not place a product under a price or promotion sign that applies to something else. Refer uncertain price questions to authorized staff.

#### Hardware-store example
Two tubes of adhesive have similar packaging but different uses. The associate checks each item against its shelf tag before filling the hooks.

#### What to say or do
- Match the product identifier to the shelf label.
- Check size and unit differences.
- Report uncertain pricing or signage.

#### Common mistakes
- Placing products based on package color.
- Handwriting a price without permission.
- Mixing items behind one label.

#### When to ask for help
Ask the pricing or merchandising lead when the tag, price, or placement does not match.

#### Supporting materials
- Optional instructional photograph or annotated shelf/product image **not included**. Add only if it demonstrates a specific point; use licensed/store-owned imagery and descriptive alt text.

#### Key takeaways
- Match the product identifier to the shelf label.
- Check size and unit differences.
- Ask the pricing or merchandising lead when the tag, price, or placement does not match.

#### Optional practice — unscored (IM-103-P02)

- C. Choose the label with the lower price.

**Answer:** B. Accurate product-to-label matching prevents confusion.

### IM-103-L03: Build Useful and Safe Displays
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Objective:** Recognize practical display principles and safety limits.

#### Why this matters
A display should make a product easy to see and select without creating a hazard. Good displays balance visibility, clear information, accessibility, and safe storage.

#### Lesson content
**Show the purpose.** A small project display can group related items, such as a paint tray, roller cover, painter’s tape, and drop cloth. Keep labels and prices clear for each product.

**Respect safe limits.** Do not stack heavy or unstable products beyond approved limits. Keep exits, safety equipment, and walking routes clear. Ask for authorized help with ladders or powered equipment.

**Keep it shoppable.** Customers should be able to take a product without pulling down a pile or disturbing another display. Replace damaged packaging through approved procedures.

#### Hardware-store example
A fall cleanup display pairs leaf bags with gloves and rakes. The team checks that the products are secure and the display does not narrow the aisle.

#### What to say or do
- Use approved display fixtures and locations.
- Keep products reachable and stable.
- Show related products with clear individual labels.

#### Common mistakes
- Blocking a walkway with promotional merchandise.
- Overloading a shelf.
- Creating a display without accurate price information.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a manager about fixture limits, heavy merchandise, or new display locations.

#### Supporting materials

#### Key takeaways

### IM-103-L04: Maintain Shelves and Seasonal Areas
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Objective:** Recognize common merchandising maintenance tasks.

#### Why this matters
Displays need attention after customers shop them. Empty hooks, mixed products, damaged packages, and outdated signs can confuse shoppers.

#### Lesson content
**Recover the aisle.** Recovery means returning products to their proper locations and straightening shelves. Check item labels as you go rather than pushing everything forward without looking.

**Check promotions.** Use the current approved promotion information. Remove or report outdated signs according to store procedure. Do not promise a promotion that cannot be verified.

**Think seasonally.** Seasonal products should be easy to find when demand rises. Follow the approved calendar and layout. Do not move stock into a new location without approval.

#### Hardware-store example
After a weekend sale, an associate finds spring seed packets mixed into fall lawn-care supplies. They sort them into approved locations and flag an expired sign.

#### What to say or do
- Check for misplaced products.
- Report empty locations and expired signs.
- Follow the approved seasonal merchandising plan.

#### Common mistakes
- Leaving an expired sale sign up.
- Moving seasonal stock without telling the team.
- Ignoring damaged display packaging.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a manager about promotional timing, seasonal resets, or unavailable replacement signs.

#### Supporting materials

#### Key takeaways

### IM-103-L05: Improve Displays Using Customer Questions
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Objective:** Use simple observations to identify display improvements.

#### Why this matters
Customers show us where merchandising is confusing. Repeated questions about a location, size, or difference between products can point to a sign or layout problem.

#### Lesson content
**Notice repeated questions.** If several shoppers cannot find the same product, check whether the location or sign is clear. One question does not prove the layout is wrong.

**Share a specific suggestion.** Describe the customer problem and a possible improvement. Example: “Three shoppers asked where hose washers are. Could we add a clearer sign near the hose fittings?”

**Make changes with approval.** Managers may consider space, inventory, brand requirements, and safety before approving a new sign or display. Do not change prices or permanent layouts on your own.

#### Hardware-store example
Customers regularly ask which sandpaper grit to choose. An associate suggests an approved comparison sign that explains grit ranges in plain language, subject to product accuracy review.

#### What to say or do
- Record the recurring customer question.
- Propose a clear, specific improvement.
- Request approval before changing fixtures or permanent signs.

#### Common mistakes
- Changing prices to make a display simpler.
- Treating one complaint as proof.
- Adding unverified technical claims to signage.

#### When to ask for help
Ask a manager to review new signs, product claims, or layout changes.

#### Supporting materials

#### Key takeaways

- Propose a clear, specific improvement.
- Ask a manager to review new signs, product claims, or layout changes.
